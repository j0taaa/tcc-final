\begin{abstract}
We develop checkable guarantees for two decisions in constrained diffusion decoding: maximizing token-commitment reward under a physical-position budget, and admitting a sampled parallel update with bounded distributional error. Resource and conflict certificates prove exact-on-support reward optima. A separate anytime partition of original token space bounds grammar-valid probability mass without counting ambiguous parses multiple times. Its sharp total-variation bound accounts for unresolved and discarded mass; byte-language coverage can certify that discarded tokens cannot be valid. The bounds transport to parallel updates and compose under explicit transition premises. Exact-rational checkers, documented Lean specifications, independent exhaustive controls and fresh CPU MDLM predictions instantiate the construction. Historical EPIC comparisons and failed transfer remain visible. The incremental contribution is certifying finite-slot inference and error-controlled admission; it guarantees neither semantic correctness nor a universally faster decoder.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Masked diffusion language models estimate several unknown positions in one forward pass \cite{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada}. Parallel proposals can nevertheless conflict: individually acceptable tokens may jointly eliminate every completion of a JSON document or program. The decoder must select a compatible batch while preserving already committed positions and the finite canvas.

A reveal budget requires optimizing the completion and committed positions jointly: selecting confidence first or filtering an unbudgeted optimum can lose arbitrarily much reward. A separate practical question is whether truncating and partially exploring predictions distorts grammar-conditioned sampling beyond an application's tolerance. We supply independently checkable certificates for both decisions, with original-token provenance, finite slots and explicit support scope. Mathematical bounds justify admission or refusal without a favorable benchmark. Fresh MDLM predictions demonstrate this interface beyond the archived replay cohort; historical negative EPIC comparisons delimit rather than establish the contribution.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:fundamentacao}

\subsection{Constrained parallel decoding}

Regular constraints admit exact inference \cite{suresh2025dingo,dang2026automata}; CFG decoding can use completion-language intersection \cite{mundler2025cfg}. EPIC accelerates validation through lexical memoization, Earley parsing and relaxed selection followed by verification \cite{jin2026epic}. Our proposal-agreement objective differs from mean-field MAP; the canonical-catalog MAP control is not a reimplementation of these decoders.

FactorDLM performs exact mean-field inference with finite-domain factor graphs and variable elimination \cite{su2026factor}. Our CFG proposal-agreement solver does not implement that decoder or claim general exact-dLLM-inference novelty.

Grammar-aligned ASAp and CARS already address constrained-sampling distortion in autoregressive LMs \cite{park2024gad,parys2025cars}. Deterministic anytime weighted model counting also predates our work \cite{dubray2024anytime,renkens2014wmc}. We specialize certificate boundaries to finite diffusion canvases; exact DFA/factor inference remains preferable when its representation is affordable. CFG ambiguity prevents a general polynomial-time probability promise \cite{amarilli2026probabilistic}.

Matching-based CLAD and anchor-based AXON already select reveal batches \cite{qi2026clad,ayoub2026axon}; confidence/trajectory gates and max-marginal filters are also established \cite{cai2026confidence,wang2026tacg,weiss2010cascades}. Our CFG objective and certificates do not reproduce these algorithms or claim general selection novelty.

\subsection{Weighted grammar--automaton intersection}

CKY/Earley parsing \cite{kasami1965,earley1970}, CFG--regular intersection \cite{barhillel1961,hopcroft2006}, weighted grammar optimization \cite{katsirelos2008weighted}, weighted intersection \cite{nederhof2003intersection,hanneforth2011intersection,pasti2023intersection} and semiring inference \cite{goodman1999semiring,mohri2002} are foundations. Max-plus optimizes a witness; it does not sum ambiguous derivations as probabilities. Prefix sharing, quality bounds and certifying DP are established \cite{xu2026trie,likhachev2003ara,demirovic2024certifying}. We specialize resource and token-provenance checking, not those principles.

\subsection{Tokenization and finite slots}

Subword vocabularies \cite{sennrich2016bpe,kudo2018sentencepiece} do not align with grammar terminals. One model token can emit several bytes or cross a lexical boundary; distinct token sequences can emit identical text. Discarding token provenance would therefore lose both proposal identity and slot accounting. Lexers may additionally impose maximal munch and priorities \cite{aho2006}. We distinguish the general lexical relation from the implemented raw-byte path, which performs no programming-language lexing.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problema}

\subsection{Token sequences and feasible completions}

Let $\vocab$ be the model vocabulary and $n$ the number of canvas slots. The current state is $x\in(\vocab\cup\{\mask\})^n$. A token completion is $y\in\vocab^n$. We say that $y$ preserves $x$ when
\begin{equation}
 \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad x_i\neq\mask \Rightarrow y_i=x_i.
\end{equation}

Let $S_i\subseteq\vocab$ be the declared alternatives in slot $i$, containing its fixed token when committed. For CFG $G=(N,\Sigma,P,S)$, let $\operatorname{emit}(y)$ concatenate token emissions into its terminal alphabet. Production uses compositional bytes; the token-aligned reference uses identity token emissions. A regular predicate $H(y)$ enforces length and EOS/PAD. Optional lexical relations are detailed in the supplement.

\begin{definition}[Feasible completion]
The set of feasible completions at state $x$ is
\begin{equation}
\Feas_x=\left\{y\in\prod_{i=1}^n S_i\;\middle|\;
y\text{ preserves }x,\ H(y),\ \operatorname{emit}(y)\in L(G)\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Proposals, weights, and compatibility}

The proposal set is treated as an indexed family
\begin{equation}
 C=(c_1,\ldots,c_m),\qquad c_j=(i_j,t_j,w_j),
\end{equation}
with position $i_j$, token $t_j\in\vocab$, and weight $w_j\geq 0$. Indexing permits multiplicities, although the common policy produces one primary proposal per position. Weights may represent transformed confidence, priority, or unit value. If they are marginal probabilities, their sum must be interpreted as preservation utility rather than the joint probability of the sequence.

For a completion $y$, define
\begin{align}
\Match_C^+(y)&=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:y_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\},\\
R_C(y)&=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j\indic[y_{i_j}=t_j].
\end{align}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility]
An index set $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ is compatible if there exists $y\in\Feas_x$ such that $y_{i_j}=t_j$ for every $j\in J$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[MWPC]
The maximum-weight parallel commitment problem is to compute
\begin{equation}
 J^*\in\argmax_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J),
 \qquad W(J)=\sum_{j\in J}w_j.
\label{eq:mwpc}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Assumptions and scope of the guarantee}

Weights must be finite and non-negative. Zero-weight proposals do not change the objective; certificates omit them and retain distinct positive duplicates. Optimization is exact only on represented alternatives: top-$K$ truncation may change feasibility and reward. Commitments also change later model distributions; this per-step guarantee implies neither full-vocabulary nor future-trajectory optimality.

\section{Finite Token Inference Foundation}
\label{sec:lattice}

For nonnegative proposals, ordinary MWPC equals
$\max_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y)$: every compatible subset is contained in the matched
positive IDs of its witness; conversely those IDs are compatible. The canonical set $J^*=\Match_C^+(y^*)$ is recovered as
$J^*\gets\{j:y^*_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\}$, including every positive duplicate. Equal scores use stable traversal; no lexicographic secondary objective is implemented or claimed.

A layered token DAG selects exactly one original token per permitted physical
slot. Byte expansion and regular EOS/PAD states retain token identity; the implementation does not insert a lexer. Max-plus CFG intersection recovers an optimal original path, while
unbounded abstract gaps would not certify a finite canvas. For an epsilon-free
graph with $q$ nodes, span parsing takes $O(|P_2|q^3+|P_1||E|)$ operations and
$O(|N|q^2)$ entries, excluding normalization. Exactness remains on represented
support. Complete ordinary MWPC equivalence, finite-slot, CKY, graph-parser and
update-invariant proofs are preserved in \path{paper/supplement-foundations.tex}.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitacoes}



Reward guarantees are per-step \texttt{exact\_on\_support}: truncation may change feasibility/reward; it is not full-vocabulary exact and does not optimize the future trajectory. \texttt{TIMEOUT} leaves feasibility and optimality unknown for reward search, unlike completed \texttt{INFEASIBLE\_ON\_SUPPORT}; large lattices may exhaust time or memory. Partial probability certificates retain sound bounds but need not authorize sampling.

The probability target is frozen mean-field, not the complete generative joint. Full-vocabulary bounds require original normalization and omitted-mass evidence; coverage currently requires absent EOS. Search can be exponential, refusal is supported, and recorded probes do not verify the trajectory theorem's every-state premises. Small specialized exact controls can be cheaper. The budgeted solver is a rational reference, not the Rust production decoder. Budgeted references, Lean specifications and independent checkers do not constitute universal source refinement; DP completeness and separation constructions remain written proofs.

ByteLevel bytes do not supply programming-language lexical semantics or factual/functional correctness. Historical decoder evidence uses finite regular catalogs, one quantized checkpoint/host, and question-derived support that may omit the answer. BFCL cases are a restricted subset; family disjointness is weaker than domain disjointness. EPIC recovery/layout affects grading, repetitions do not enlarge the sample, and secondary comparisons are exploratory. Unequal proposal budgets confound some historical timings. EOS/PAD reward can inflate scores. GPU peak includes weights; historical live CPU peak is unmeasured. Repair excludes general edits/naturally observed model errors. These limitations remain separate from the stated mathematical guarantees.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusao}

Finite-token certificates prove a maximum-reward batch or authorize a grammar-valid sampled update at a declared distributional tolerance. Exact budget frontiers, separation proofs, safe reuse and ambiguity-safe mass envelopes justify these decisions mathematically. Coverage can eliminate valid omitted mass without hiding truncation. Fresh MDLM executions exercise admission and refusal; the larger-canvas audit finds declining admission and cheaper compact exact inference. The contribution is incremental certification and integration of established ideas, with production benefit and priority unestablished. Lean's documented specifications complement independent implementation checks; scalable probability search, production budgeted Rust and full source refinement remain future work.
