\begin{abstract}
Parallel dLLM proposals can jointly violate a grammar. We formulate maximum-weight parallel commitment (MWPC) as highest-reward feasible completion. Independent Python/Rust solvers preserve finite slots, byte provenance and checked certificates; optimality is per step on represented support. With quantized LLaDA-8B-Instruct on 100 new synthetic tool requests, four-proposal MWPC produces \MLiveBudgetFourCorrect{} correct calls versus \MLiveEpicEightCorrect{} for eight-step EPIC, at median total latencies of \MLiveBudgetFourMs{} and \MLiveEpicEightMs{} ms. This secondary comparison includes upstream recovery; whitespace normalization reduces the difference to eight points. The frozen confidence comparison is faster than EPIC 24 but does not establish accuracy non-inferiority. On 42 external queries the primary reaches \MGroundedConfidenceCorrect{} correct calls versus \MGroundedEpicThirtyTwoCorrect{} for EPIC 32, limiting transfer. MAP/greedy controls and read-only dispatch delimit scope. The contribution is a certified integration and reproducible cost--quality evidence, not a new parsing algorithm or universal superiority.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Masked diffusion language models estimate several unknown positions in one forward pass \cite{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada}. Parallel proposals can nevertheless conflict: individually acceptable tokens may jointly eliminate every completion of a JSON document or program. The decoder must select a compatible batch while preserving already committed positions and the finite canvas.

This work finds the maximum-weight compatible batch with a max-plus parse. For non-negative weights, each completion determines all proposals it matches, eliminating subset enumeration. The contribution combines a per-step formulation, a finite lattice preserving token provenance, independently validated certificates, and evidence from real dLLM tool calls. Geographic dispatch illustrates the application path; model-free JSON repair remains secondary. Optimality is on declared support, without full-vocabulary or future-trajectory guarantees.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:fundamentacao}

\subsection{Constrained parallel decoding}

A reveal policy proposes tokens from masked-position marginals; serial acceptance preserves feasibility but is order-dependent.

DINGO uses dynamic programming for regular-language constraints \cite{suresh2025dingo}; Dang and Ermon study exact inference over mean-field distributions conditioned by finite automata \cite{dang2026automata}. For CFGs, Mündler et al. test completion feasibility through intersection with a regular representation of partial outputs \cite{mundler2025cfg}. EPIC accelerates this process with lexical memoization, graph-based Earley validation and relaxed batch selection followed by verification \cite{jin2026epic}. MWPC replaces repeated subset checks with one weighted inference on the represented finite support. Its proposal-agreement objective differs from constrained mean-field MAP: maximizing selected proposal weights is not maximizing the product of all token probabilities. We therefore include an exhaustive canonical-catalog MAP control, without identifying it with a full reimplementation of DINGO or Dang and Ermon.

FactorDLM performs exact mean-field inference with finite-domain factor graphs and variable elimination \cite{su2026factor}. Our CFG proposal-agreement solver does not implement that decoder or claim general exact-dLLM-inference novelty.

CLAD performs exact weighted selection on matching conflicts from attention clusters \cite{qi2026clad}, rather than CFG-valid completion. Exact parallel subset selection is therefore not a general novelty claim here.

Confidence gating and trajectory-based commitment are established \cite{cai2026confidence,wang2026tacg}. Our threshold control around a grammar-certified witness does not reproduce their algorithms or sampling guarantees. Max-marginal filtering predates dLLMs \cite{weiss2010cascades}; our counterfactual margins do not claim to invent it.

\subsection{Weighted grammar--automaton intersection}

CFGs express recursion, including nested arrays and balanced delimiters. CKY parses normalized grammars over spans; Earley supports general productions \cite{kasami1965,earley1970}. Intersecting a CFG with a regular language preserves context-freeness \cite{barhillel1961,hopcroft2006}. Weighted CFG optimization predates dLLMs \cite{katsirelos2008weighted}. Weighted intersection is also established: Nederhof and Satta formulate probabilistic parsing as intersection, and Hanneforth presents a practical weighted algorithm \cite{nederhof2003intersection,hanneforth2011intersection}. Pasti et al. extend the construction to epsilon arcs while preserving structural correspondence \cite{pasti2023intersection}. Our finite DAG implementation instead uses maximum-weight epsilon closure with original-path backpointers; it needs one optimal witness, not an encoding of all derivations.

Semiring parsing separates chart structure from its arithmetic \cite{goodman1999semiring,mohri2002}. Here max-plus uses $\max$ for alternatives, addition for composition, $-\infty$ for impossibility and zero for an empty reward. This maximizes one completion rather than summing probability mass over all CFG words, whose complexity can depend on ambiguity \cite{amarilli2026probabilistic}. Neutral grammar-production weights ensure that multiple derivations do not multiply a token's reward.

\subsection{Tokenization and finite slots}

Subword vocabularies \cite{sennrich2016bpe,kudo2018sentencepiece} do not align with grammar terminals. One model token can emit several bytes or cross a lexical boundary; distinct token sequences can emit identical text. Discarding token provenance would therefore lose both proposal identity and slot accounting. Lexers may additionally impose maximal munch and priorities \cite{aho2006}. We distinguish the general lexical relation from the implemented raw-byte path, which performs no programming-language lexing.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problema}

\subsection{Token sequences and feasible completions}

Let $\vocab$ be the model vocabulary and $n$ the number of canvas slots. The current state is $x\in(\vocab\cup\{\mask\})^n$. A token completion is $y\in\vocab^n$. We say that $y$ preserves $x$ when
\begin{equation}
 \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad x_i\neq\mask \Rightarrow y_i=x_i.
\end{equation}

Let $S_i\subseteq\vocab$ be the declared alternatives in slot $i$, containing its fixed token when committed. For CFG $G=(N,\Sigma,P,S)$, let $\operatorname{emit}(y)$ concatenate token emissions into its terminal alphabet. Production uses compositional bytes; the token-aligned reference uses identity token emissions. A regular predicate $H(y)$ enforces length and EOS/PAD. Optional lexical relations are detailed in the supplement.

\begin{definition}[Feasible completion]
The set of feasible completions at state $x$ is
\begin{equation}
\Feas_x=\left\{y\in\prod_{i=1}^n S_i\;\middle|\;
y\text{ preserves }x,\ H(y),\ \operatorname{emit}(y)\in L(G)\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Positions, model tokens and emitted terminals remain distinct, even when different token sequences emit the same bytes.

\subsection{Proposals, weights, and compatibility}

The proposal set is treated as an indexed family
\begin{equation}
 C=(c_1,\ldots,c_m),\qquad c_j=(i_j,t_j,w_j),
\end{equation}
with position $i_j$, token $t_j\in\vocab$, and weight $w_j\geq 0$. Indexing permits multiplicities, although the common policy produces one primary proposal per position. Weights may represent transformed confidence, priority, or unit value. If they are marginal probabilities, their sum must be interpreted as preservation utility rather than the joint probability of the sequence.

For a completion $y$, define
\begin{align}
\Match_C^+(y)&=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:y_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\},\\
R_C(y)&=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j\indic[y_{i_j}=t_j].
\end{align}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility]
An index set $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ is compatible if there exists $y\in\Feas_x$ such that $y_{i_j}=t_j$ for every $j\in J$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[MWPC]
The maximum-weight parallel commitment problem is to compute
\begin{equation}
 J^*\in\argmax_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J),
 \qquad W(J)=\sum_{j\in J}w_j.
\label{eq:mwpc}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Explicit search examines up to $2^m$ sets. The problem, however, has additional structure: every compatible set has a completion as a witness, and every completion defines the proposals it matches.

\subsection{Assumptions and scope of the guarantee}

Weights must be finite and non-negative. Zero-weight proposals do not change the objective; certificates omit them and retain distinct positive duplicates. Optimization is exact only on represented alternatives: top-$K$ truncation may change feasibility and reward. Commitments also change later model distributions; this per-step guarantee implies neither full-vocabulary nor future-trajectory optimality.

\section{Reduction to the Highest-Reward Feasible Completion}
\label{sec:reducao}

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental equivalence]
If $w_j\geq 0$ for every $j$, then
\begin{equation}
\max_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J)
=
\max_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y).
\label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}
If $\Feas_x=\varnothing$, both problems are declared infeasible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any $y\in\Feas_x$, $\Match_C^+(y)$ is compatible with witness $y$ and has weight $R_C(y)$, since omitted matched proposals have zero weight. Therefore the left maximum is at least the right maximum. Conversely, a compatible $J$ has a witness $y$. Its positive-weight indices $J^+=\{j\in J:w_j>0\}$ satisfy $J^+\subseteq\Match_C^+(y)$, so non-negativity gives $W(J)=W(J^+)\leq W(\Match_C^+(y))=R_C(y)$. Taking maxima gives the opposite inequality and equality follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal-batch recovery]
If $y^*\in\argmax_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y)$, then the canonical batch $J^*=\Match_C^+(y^*)$ solves MWPC.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$J^*$ is compatible because $y^*$ is its witness. Its weight is $R_C(y^*)$, which equals the MWPC optimum by Theorem~1.
\end{proof}

The theorem eliminates the need to represent subsets in the search space. The parser must find one valid completion whose reward is additive by position or arc. Backtracking that completion simultaneously provides a concrete compatibility certificate and the optimal set.

The grammar need not be unambiguous: reward is attached to tokens or arcs and productions are neutral, so derivations of one completion have equal value. Equal scores use stable traversal; no lexicographic secondary objective is implemented or claimed.

\subsection{A worked commitment example}

Let $G$ generate exactly \texttt{ayz} and \texttt{xbc}, and let the three-slot canvas be entirely masked. Consider proposals $(1,\mathtt{a},4)$, $(2,\mathtt{b},3)$, and $(3,\mathtt{c},2)$. Each is individually feasible. The first proposal is incompatible with either of the others, whereas the second and third can coexist. The two completions score 4 and 5, respectively; thus the optimum is \texttt{xbc}, committing the last two proposals. A greedy policy that accepts the highest-weight proposal first retains only weight 4. The completion certifies compatibility; the decoder need not commit every token it contains.

Slot accounting is a separate issue. With one remaining slot whose tokens emit only \texttt{a} or \texttt{b}, the grammar $S\rightarrow\mathtt{a}\mathtt{b}$ is infeasible. An abstract $\Sigma^*$ gap accepts \texttt{ab} and gives a false positive. If the tokenizer also contains a single token emitting \texttt{ab} and that token is represented in the slot, the same grammar becomes feasible. The bound counts token choices, not emitted characters.

\section{Token-Aligned Algorithm}
\label{sec:tokenaligned}

In this version, each model token is a CFG terminal and $H$ requires only length $n$. EOS/PAD restrictions are composed in the lattice version. Assume that $G$ is in Chomsky normal form, with productions $A\rightarrow BC$ and $A\rightarrow a$. Unit productions, empty productions, and special symbols must be removed or handled by a separate closure.

For each position $i$ and terminal $a$, define the lexical reward
\begin{equation}
s_i(a)=
\begin{cases}
\neginf, & a\notin S_i\text{ or }(x_i\neq\mask\text{ and }a\neq x_i),\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j:i_j=i,\ t_j=a}w_j, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:lexscore}
\end{equation}
The empty sum is zero. Thus, tokens outside the declared support or incompatible with fixed positions are impossible, while permitted tokens receive exactly the sum of the proposals they satisfy.

Define $D[A,i,j]$ as the highest reward of a string derivable from $A$ that occupies positions $i,\ldots,j-1$. The base cases are
\begin{equation}
D[A,i,i+1]=\max_{A\rightarrow a\in P}s_i(a).
\label{eq:basecky}
\end{equation}
For longer spans,
\begin{equation}
D[A,i,j]=
\max_{A\rightarrow BC\in P}\ \max_{i<k<j}
\left(D[B,i,k]+D[C,k,j]\right).
\label{eq:cky}
\end{equation}
Impossible entries have value $\neginf$. Backpointers store the terminal, production, and split that attain each maximum.



\begin{theorem}[Correctness of weighted CKY]
For every entry $D[A,i,j]$ computed by Equations~\ref{eq:basecky}--\ref{eq:cky}, its value is the maximum of $\sum_{r=i}^{j-1}s_r(y_r)$ over strings $y_i\cdots y_{j-1}$ derivable from $A$. Consequently, the algorithm returns a maximum-reward completion $y^*\in\Feas_x$ and an optimal MWPC batch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof is by induction on length $j-i$. For length one, Equation~\ref{eq:basecky} examines every terminal production and assigns the correct score. For a longer span, every CNF derivation begins with some production $A\rightarrow BC$ and splits the string at $k$. By the induction hypothesis, both child entries contain the best values for their subspans; adding them yields the best derivation for that production and split. Taking the maximum over all choices covers every possible derivation. The start entry therefore represents the highest-reward feasible completion. Corollary~1 converts that completion into the optimal batch.
\end{proof}

If $|P_2|$ is the number of binary productions, a direct implementation takes $O(|P_2|n^3+|P_1|n)$ time and $O(|N|n^2)$ memory, in addition to backpointers. This version is the reference implementation: its output can be compared with enumeration of all subsets and all strings on small instances.

\section{Generalization to a Finite Tokenizer-Aware Lattice}
\label{sec:lattice}

\subsection{Token lattice and provenance}

A \emph{weighted token lattice} is a layered directed acyclic graph $A_{\mathrm{tok}}=(Q,E,q_0,F,\omega,\pi)$. For a canvas without early EOS, $Q$ contains layers $0,\ldots,n$; every arc from layer $i-1$ to $i$ corresponds to a token permitted in slot $i$. If $x_i$ is fixed, only its arc exists. If it is masked, there is one arc for every token in the represented support $S_i\subseteq\vocab$.

The weight of an arc labeled $a$ in slot $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\omega(i,a)=\sum_{j:i_j=i,\ t_j=a}w_j.
\end{equation}
The function $\pi(i,a)$ stores the indices of positive-weight proposals satisfied by that arc. A complete path chooses exactly one token per slot, and the sum of its weights is $R_C(y)$. Provenance recovers the canonical $J^*=\Match_C^+(y^*)$ without inferring it again from detokenized text.

When the model permits early termination, the lattice is composed with a regular constraint that accepts one occurrence of $\eos$ followed only by $\pad$, or it uses final states at different layers under explicit semantics. In either case, the number of consumed token arcs remains tied to the real canvas slots.

\subsection{Composition with detokenization and lexing}

EOS/PAD conditions apply to token IDs before detokenization. Expanding each token arc to bytes preserves its weight and provenance. The implemented CFG consumes raw-byte terminals; it does not insert a lexer or claim maximal-munch semantics. Optional composition with a lexical transducer must implement its declared priorities rather than accept arbitrary segmentation. That reference interface and its composition equation are detailed in the supplement and remain unused in live experiments.

\subsection{Max-plus parsing over the graph}

Let $Q$ be the state set of the resulting lexical graph. For a CFG in CNF, define
\begin{equation}
D[A,p,q]=\text{maximum weight of a path from $p$ to $q$ derivable from $A$}.
\end{equation}
For each terminal arc $e=(p,a,q)$,
\begin{equation}
D[A,p,q]\gets\max(D[A,p,q],\omega(e))
\quad\text{for every }A\rightarrow a.
\end{equation}
For binary productions,
\begin{equation}
D[A,p,q]=
\max_{A\rightarrow BC}\ \max_{r\in Q}
\left(D[B,p,r]+D[C,r,q]\right).
\label{eq:graphdp}
\end{equation}
The algorithm is the Viterbi version of CFG--automaton intersection. A weighted Earley implementation may avoid CNF and exploit top-down guidance, but it must preserve the same maximum and backpointer semantics.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Max-plus parsing over a finite lattice}
\label{alg:graph}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require strict CNF $G$ with flag $\nu=[\varepsilon\in L(G)]$; finite weighted DAG $A$
\Ensure status, best original path, provenance, and score
\State $(\bar A,\rho,z_\varepsilon)\gets\Call{NormalizeEpsilon}{A}$ \Comment{see text}
\State Topologically index states; initialize every $D[B,p,q]\gets\neginf$
\State Seed terminal entries using saturated arc weights and edge backpointers
\State Apply recurrence~\eqref{eq:graphdp} by increasing span, storing each winning split
\State $z\gets$ best reconstructed $D[S,q_{\mathrm{start}},f]$ over $f\in F$, or none
\State Expand $z$ through $\rho$; compare with $z_\varepsilon$ when $\nu$ permits it
\If{neither candidate exists} \State \Return \textsc{InfeasibleOnSupport} \EndIf
\State Recover tokens $y^*$ from the winning original path
\State $J^*\gets\{j:y^*_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\}$
\State \Return \textsc{Optimal}, witness, proposal IDs, score, support specification
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Here strict CNF contains only terminal and binary productions; a separate flag records empty-word acceptance. Grammar normalization removes nullable and unit productions before parsing. \textsc{NormalizeEpsilon} computes maximum-weight epsilon-only paths between state pairs in the DAG. For each terminal arc, it creates saturated arcs consisting of an epsilon prefix, that terminal, and an epsilon suffix. Each saturated arc retains its original edge sequence in $\rho$; $z_\varepsilon$ is the best entirely epsilon path from the start to any final state, including a zero-edge path when permitted. Comparing it separately is necessary when a canvas emits only EOS/PAD.

Any original nonempty path can be partitioned around its terminal edges; replacing an epsilon segment by the maximum between the same endpoints cannot reduce its score. Conversely, concatenated saturated arcs expand to an existing original DAG path with the same weight. Equal-score alternatives may share a representative because the objective requires one optimal witness, not every optimum. Original edge IDs preserve token provenance. A completed search alone can return \textsc{Optimal} or \textsc{InfeasibleOnSupport}; a deadline interruption returns \textsc{Timeout} without a certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Lattice soundness, completeness, and optimality]
Assume that: (i) the lattice represents exactly the token sequences permitted by the state and $H$; (ii) terminal expansion represents exactly $\operatorname{emit}$; (iii) grammar normalization, epsilon saturation, and the separate empty-output candidate preserve maximum scores and reconstructible original paths; and (iv) the span dynamic program completes without unsafe pruning. Then Algorithm~\ref{alg:graph}: (a) returns only a completion in $\Feas_x$; (b) finds a completion whenever $\Feas_x$ is nonempty on the represented support; and (c) returns a maximum-weight MWPC batch on that support.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every reconstructed original path starts at $q_0$, ends at a final state, and follows existing arcs. By assumption (i), it corresponds to a permitted token sequence; by assumption (ii) and the derivation from $S$, its emitted terminals belong to $L(G)$. This proves soundness. For completeness, every feasible completion corresponds to a lattice path and some CFG derivation, recovered by epsilon normalization and the terminal/binary rules; entirely epsilon paths are covered by the separate candidate. For optimality, the induction used in Theorem~2 generalizes spans to state pairs: each entry contains the maximum over all compatible paths and derivations. Comparing the best start entry with the empty-output candidate yields $\max_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y)$, and Theorem~1 yields MWPC.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-slot exactness]
If every complete path through $A_{\mathrm{tok}}$ traverses exactly one token arc per permitted slot and composition neither creates nor removes token arcs, every returned witness is representable in the actual number of canvas positions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the layered construction, a start-to-finish path chooses one arc from layer $i-1$ to $i$ for each slot $i$. Internal detokenization or lexing transitions may emit zero or more symbols, but remain associated with the same token macro-arcs. Thus, no accepted path uses more slots than exist or depends on an abstract unbounded region.
\end{proof}

For an explicit epsilon-free graph with $q=|Q|$, the span parser takes $O(|P_2|q^3+|P_1||E|)$ time and $O(|N|q^2)$ chart memory. This excludes grammar normalization and epsilon saturation; $|E|$ counts saturated edges, which may greatly outnumber the original arcs. A naive token lattice may have $O(n|\vocab|)$ arcs. Polynomial complexity therefore does not imply low cost. With top-$K$ support, optimality holds only over the represented alternatives $S_i$.

\section{Iterative Decoder and Progress Guarantee}
\label{sec:decoder}

The optimizer solves one step, but the decoder must define proposals, budget, ties, fallback, and termination. In the policy study, its complete selected set remains in the certificate while a separate gate commits a subset. A confidence threshold applies to raw full-vocabulary model probabilities; it is not a calibrated probability of semantic correctness. Committing fewer witness-matching tokens preserves feasibility but changes the future model trajectory. The implemented schedule restricts $C$ to at most $k_s$ eligible positions before MWPC, with one primary proposal per position; a cardinality dimension inside the parser remains an unimplemented extension.

An important case is $J^*=\varnothing$ even though a valid completion exists. The decoder cannot stall. Witness $y^*$ provides a safe progress fallback: committing its token at one masked position preserves that witness as feasible. This token may not be the model's primary proposal, but it guarantees progress without violating the grammar.



\begin{proposition}[Completion-feasibility invariant]
If $x$ has a feasible witness and a step commits only tokens matching a witness returned by the parser, then the new state still has a feasible witness.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The witness used at the step already preserves every fixed position in $x$. Since the new commitments match it, the witness also preserves the new state and continues to satisfy the lexical, grammatical, and slot conditions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Termination]
Assume that the canvas has finitely many masks, remasking is disabled, every optimizer call returns a feasible witness, and every step commits at least one position. If no earlier step-budget stop is imposed, the decoder completes after at most the initial number of masks, and the final sequence belongs to the constrained language.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The number of masks strictly decreases and cannot be negative. At the final state, the witness that maintains the invariant must match every fixed token, so the output itself is valid.
\end{proof}

\section{Implementation and Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:metodologia}

EPIC remains read-only at \texttt{5b1b310}; \texttt{serial}, \texttt{epic} and \texttt{exact} remain available. Python implements the readable reference, support construction and independent validation; Rust implements the production parser through thin PyO3 bindings. Parent software and research artifacts use MIT; manuscript rights and upstream notices remain separate. Every measured cohort records its source commit, immutable configuration, seed, grammar/support hashes, model/tokenizer revisions, hardware and statuses. Full traces and generating scripts are versioned separately from derived tables.

The historical local submission release \texttt{v0.2.0} remains available; subsequent studies retain their own producing commits.

Normalization produces strict CNF and an empty-word flag; epsilon closure retains original paths. The LLaDA tokenizer reverses ByteLevel pieces to compositional bytes, including split UTF-8 characters. EOS/PAD consumes physical slots without emitting bytes. Fixed rows are singletons; independent checks validate witness acceptance, provenance, selected proposals and recomputed score. Python rational sums and Rust integer-scaled sums compare binary64 weights without intermediate-rounding order changes; the public score remains a float. Arithmetic-operation bounds do not imply constant bit cost. Detailed contracts and historical configurations remain in \path{paper/supplement-method.tex}.

\subsection{Correctness, selection quality and scaling}

Exhaustive completion/subset oracles compare the Python and Rust objectives, allowing different optimal witnesses. Tests cover duplicate proposals, invalid weights, finite-slot counterexamples, empty outputs, fixed positions, timeouts and byte provenance. Recursive synthetic configurations measure possible heuristic loss; saved real-model states compare selectors on identical logits, canvases and supports. Runtime replays aggregate repetitions within each state. Their score and selector-time results are distinguished from end-to-end task success.

The recursive generation study uses twelve development and twelve disjoint confirmation prompts for brackets, arithmetic and JSON, with 32 slots and matched initial proposal budgets. Its incomplete and incorrect outputs remain failures. Controlled minimum-substitution JSON repair is a separate model-free application: one proposal per original token minimizes represented substitutions while preserving protected spans. \subsection{Live commitment-policy comparison}

All new live studies use \texttt{GSAI-ML/LLaDA-8B-Instruct}, revision \texttt{08b83a6}, NF4 double quantization with bfloat16 compute, on an RTX 3080 Ti and one CPU thread. Two warm-ups precede measurement. CUDA is synchronized; loading is excluded and support/grammar preparation plus EPIC's official recovery are included. Method order rotates by request and is reversed in the second confirmation execution. Model forward, candidate construction, support construction, selection and update times are separately recorded. Repetitions measure timing stability, not additional independent tasks.

The synthetic tool study translates requests to nested calculator calls without evaluating away requested operations. Thirty requests develop policies; 100 new requests from the same templates form confirmation. A fixed call grammar and question-derived operand filter never read the expected call. Each method receives 24 canvas slots and at most 24 model forwards. MWPC takes the highest-probability allowed proposal at each free position, with weights normalized over the model's full vocabulary. Budget policies restrict this proposal set before solving. Confidence policies keep the optimizer's full certificate but commit only matched tokens above threshold $\tau$, falling back to one matched proposal or a witness token to make progress.

Controls include confidence-greedy selection with witness reuse, actual upstream EPIC with several reveal schedules, and canonical-catalog mean-field MAP using sums of token log probabilities. MAP maximizes a different objective and is explicitly limited to canonical token paths; the byte CFG can accept other represented tokenizations. EPIC uses its native vocabulary, lexical whitespace and abstract gaps, so it is a system comparison rather than an identical-support optimality comparison. A matched greedy/exact comparison isolates selection under shared supports.

The frozen primary comparison is MWPC with $\tau=0.8$ versus EPIC with 24 steps. We report paired correctness differences, a conservative 95\% interval from exact win/loss binomial intervals, exact McNemar tests and paired latency ratios with 2,000 request-level bootstrap resamples. Secondary policy/schedule comparisons have no multiplicity adjustment. Strict grading preserves the requested syntax tree; a declared sensitivity check removes lexical layout differences while preserving quoted string contents. Numeric equivalence is reported separately.

\subsection{External grounded calls and practical dispatch}

Eight enum-only BFCL cases were too narrow to support external generalization. The extension audits 658 public records at pinned Gorilla revision \texttt{6ea5797} \cite{bfcldataset}. Eligibility uses schemas only: one query-named function, one to three scalar arguments and exactly one unrestricted string field. All 68 eligible cases are retained. Hash-ordering function-name families assigns 26 requests from eight families to development and 42 requests from 42 other families to confirmation; this does not guarantee unrelated semantic domains.

The free field receives at most 96 deterministic candidates from quoted/capitalized spans and contiguous question spans up to six words, plus schema defaults. Numeric values come from the request or defaults, with a declared 0--10 fallback; enums and optional omissions follow the schema. The cap is 4,096 canonical calls and 64 slots. All 68 catalogs fit, with at most 43 ordinary tokens. Only after construction, an independent coverage audit finds acceptable answers in 56 of the 68 supports; the other twelve remain evaluation failures. A strict scalar AST checker evaluates original schemas and public acceptable values. This is not the official BFCL score or unrestricted argument generation.

The protocol retains strong EPIC schedules, matched greedy and iterative/one-pass MAP controls, at most 64 forwards, 120 seconds per generation and ten seconds per selection. A local demonstration uses the same decoder to produce a geographic lookup, validates the AST and dispatches only a read-only Open-Meteo query \cite{openmeteogeocoding}. Live inference and archived replay are explicit modes. Such a runnable flow establishes integration, not population accuracy or a need for an LLM in geocoding itself.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitacoes}



All optimality claims are per-step \texttt{exact\_on\_support}, over finite represented alternatives. Q5's top-$K$ omits most vocabulary items, possibly changing feasibility or objective; it is not full-vocabulary exact and does not optimize the future trajectory, joint probability, or later logits. At a configured deadline, \texttt{TIMEOUT} leaves feasibility and optimality unknown and carries no certificate, unlike completed \texttt{INFEASIBLE\_ON\_SUPPORT} for the recorded lattice; larger lattices may exhaust time or memory.

The tokenizer contract is pinned ByteLevel bytes, not singleton-\texttt{decode} Unicode; added controls are unsupported except EOS/PAD. No programming-language lexer is integrated into MWPC. A CFG cannot guarantee factual or functional correctness. The main end-to-end benefit uses synthetic templates, finite catalogs, one quantized checkpoint and one host. A question-derived support can omit the right argument. Free scalar BFCL cases broaden the test but do not constitute the full benchmark; function-name disjointness is weaker than domain disjointness. The measured finite languages are regular, so they do not demonstrate an expressivity advantage for CFGs.

Strict output grading is sensitive to EPIC's lexical layout, hence the separate normalization analysis. EPIC's official recovery can select different completions between processes despite a fixed seed. Repetitions do not enlarge the independent sample; secondary comparisons are exploratory. EOS/PAD rewards may inflate content-relevant scores. Greedy rebuilds charts for unmatched proposals; incremental Boolean parsing is untested. Peak GPU allocation includes weights and CPU peak memory is not measured in the live policy study. Repair remains structural substitution over a restricted schema, without general insertions/deletions or naturally observed model errors.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusao}

A highest-reward feasible completion identifies a maximum-weight compatible proposal set for non-negative weights. The contribution combines finite slots, byte provenance, independently verifiable certificates and reproducible dLLM evidence. Selecting an optimum and deciding how much to reveal remain separate: conservative commitment benefits the synthetic operating point, but external calls favor EPIC/MAP and aggressive commitment can harm later predictions. Weighted parsing, confidence gates and constrained inference are established foundations. Wider supports, multiple checkpoints, incremental baselines and policy calibration are future work. Geographic dispatch demonstrates integration while keeping structural guarantees distinct from intent accuracy.
