\begin{abstract}
Parallel token proposals in diffusion language models may be individually plausible but jointly incompatible with a context-free grammar. This work formulates maximum-weight parallel commitment (MWPC) as weighted parsing: for non-negative proposal weights, the best compatible subset has the same value as the highest-reward valid completion. A max-plus parser recovers an optimal witness and all positive-weight proposals it matches. The implementation combines independent Python and Rust solvers with a finite token lattice, compositional byte detokenization, EOS/PAD accounting, and independent certificate validation. Its guarantee is per step and exact on the represented support. Oracle comparisons agreed in \MThirteenQOneCases{} original cases and \MReviewBranchingCases{} recursive configurations. A matched-budget LLaDA pilot on twelve nonliteral tasks exposed a practical limitation: the exact strategy completed only one generation within the step budget and achieved no functional success. These results support the formulation and implementation while leaving an end-to-end advantage unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive language models generate a sequence from left to right, conditioning each new token on the previously generated prefix. Discrete diffusion language models use a different organization: they start from a corrupted sequence, often composed of masks, and perform successive denoising steps that estimate tokens for several still-unknown positions. Discrete diffusion processes were studied by \cite{austin2021d3pm}; simpler and more scalable masked-diffusion formulations were proposed by \cite{sahoo2024mdlm}; and larger models such as LLaDA showed that the approach can be applied to the large-language-model paradigm \cite{nie2025llada}. The ability to update several positions in one pass is a principal motivation for this paradigm.

Parallel generation also makes structural constraints harder to enforce. In code, JSON, expressions, domain-specific languages, and other formal outputs, validity is not an isolated property of each position. Two tokens may each be acceptable in isolation while becoming incompatible when fixed together. An autoregressive model can filter its next tokens from the current prefix. In a dLLM, masks remain in several regions, tokens may be revealed out of order, and every decision must preserve at least one valid completion of the entire canvas.

DINGO demonstrated constrained inference under regular languages through dynamic programming \cite{suresh2025dingo}. Later, \cite{mundler2025cfg} reduced CFG-constrained decoding to checking whether a partial output remains completable. Their method translates the partial state into a regular language of possible completions and tests whether its intersection with the CFG language is nonempty. EPIC reduced the cost of this approach through lexical memoization, Earley validation directly over a graph, and relaxed subset selection that recovers part of the parallelism \cite{jin2026epic}. However, when a full batch fails, identifying the best proposal subset remains a combinatorial choice handled by a heuristic policy.

This work asks: \emph{can we select exactly, without enumerating every subset, the maximum-weight set of parallel proposals that still admits a representable CFG-valid completion?} The central hypothesis is that enumeration over $2^{|C|}$ is unnecessary. Every valid completion automatically determines which proposals it preserves. Subset search can therefore be replaced by maximum-weight derivation search using weighted parsing and the max-plus semiring \cite{goodman1999semiring,mohri2002}.

The overall goal is to develop and demonstrate an exact parallel-commitment algorithm for CFG-constrained dLLMs. The specific goals are: (i) formalize \emph{Maximum-Weight Parallel Commitment} (MWPC); (ii) prove its equivalence to the highest-reward feasible completion; (iii) construct a token-aligned max-plus CKY version; (iv) generalize it to a finite lattice that preserves tokenization, lexical analysis, and provenance; (v) prove soundness, completeness, per-step optimality, and \emph{finite-slot exactness}; and (vi) implement and evaluate the method on finite generated instances and matched-budget nonliteral tasks.

Weighted parsing is established in the literature. The contribution is the formulation of dLLM parallel commitment as a single weighted inference, the tokenizer-aware construction with finite slots, and its integration into an iterative decoder. The claimed optimality is local to the current step: the method maximizes the defined objective over the current proposals and possible completions of the current state. It does not claim to optimize the entire future trajectory because subsequent logits depend on the commitments made.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:fundamentacao}

\subsection{Diffusion language models and parallel commitment}

Consider a finite token vocabulary $\vocab$ and a canvas of length $n$. A partial state is
\begin{equation}
 x \in (\vocab \cup \{\mask\})^n.
\end{equation}
At step $s$, the model estimates marginal distributions $p_\theta^{(s)}(\cdot\mid x)$ for masked positions. A reveal policy chooses proposed positions and tokens, usually from confidence, entropy, or the denoising schedule. Committing a proposal permanently replaces a mask with the corresponding token unless the sampler allows remasking.

Parallelism creates a distinction between \emph{proposing} and \emph{committing}. A model forward pass can propose many tokens simultaneously. Under a global constraint, however, the decoder must decide which proposals can become irrevocable without eliminating every valid completion. Serial validation tests one proposal, updates the state, and continues to the next; this is correct when every acceptance preserves a witness, but its outcome depends on order and may discard a better batch.

\subsection{Regular and context-free constraints}

Regular languages are representable by finite automata and capture local patterns, regular expressions, and many fixed schemas. DINGO formulates constrained inference for dLLMs under this class and uses dynamic programming to find or sample accepted sequences \cite{suresh2025dingo}. Later work provides exact inference over a mean-field distribution conditioned by finite automata, including sampling and greedy decoding \cite{dang2026automata}.

A context-free grammar $G=(N,\Sigma,P,S)$ has nonterminals $N$, terminals $\Sigma$, productions $P$, and start symbol $S$. CFGs represent recursive dependencies such as balanced parentheses, nested blocks, and syntax trees. CKY string recognition for a grammar in Chomsky normal form uses a chart over spans and is cubic in input length \cite{kasami1965}. Earley recognizes general CFGs without the same normalization and also has a cubic worst-case bound \cite{earley1970}. The intersection of a context-free language with a regular language remains context-free and can be constructed with states that combine nonterminals and pairs of automaton states \cite{barhillel1961,hopcroft2006}.

\subsection{Weighted parsing and semirings}

Parsing algorithms can be parameterized by a semiring. In the Boolean semiring, addition represents disjunction and multiplication represents conjunction, so the chart records reachability only. In the tropical max-plus semiring,
\begin{equation}
 (\R\cup\{\neginf\},\;\oplus=\max,\;\otimes=+,\;\mathbf{0}=\neginf,\;\mathbf{1}=0),
\end{equation}
the chart records the highest-scoring derivation. This abstraction unifies recognition, Viterbi, inside, and other quantities \cite{goodman1999semiring}. Weighted parsing algorithms and graph structures follow the same algebraic principles \cite{goodman1999semiring,mohri2002}.

This problem uses max-plus rather than sum-product. The distinction matters for the problem studied by \cite{amarilli2026probabilistic}: computing the total mass of CFG words under independent distributions may require unambiguity assumptions and become \#P-hard for general classes. Here, contributions from all words or derivations are not summed; only the best completion is sought. Provided that production weights do not artificially count ambiguity, multiple derivations of the same string do not change its maximum reward.

\subsection{Tokenization and the lexical interface}

Models operate over subwords produced by techniques such as BPE \cite{sennrich2016bpe} or SentencePiece \cite{kudo2018sentencepiece}. Programming-language grammars, by contrast, often use characters or categories such as \texttt{IDENTIFIER}, \texttt{NUMBER}, and \texttt{LPAREN}. One token may contain several characters, cross lexical boundaries, or complete a lexeme begun by another token. An exact method must simultaneously preserve model-token identity, emitted symbols, and the number of consumed slots.

If a program detokenizes first and discards provenance, two tokenizations that produce the same text become indistinguishable even when only one matches the proposal at a position. This work instead retains each choice as an arc in a token lattice and composes it with a detokenization and lexing relation. The lexical-analysis concepts of maximal munch, rule priority, and keywords versus identifiers follow compiler literature \cite{aho2006}.

\subsection{CFG-constrained decoding for dLLMs}

\cite{mundler2025cfg} define completion feasibility for a partial state: at least one way of filling the masks must produce an output in the target language. Their procedure builds a regular representation of possible completions of the partial output and decides whether its intersection with the CFG is nonempty. This formulation supports outputs with masks in several regions, but repeated validation introduces cost.

EPIC addresses three bottlenecks: repeated lexing, automaton determinization/minimization, and lost parallelism from serial checks \cite{jin2026epic}. For the last, it proposes a regular cover of the CFG and a heuristic split of the batch, followed by exact validation and serial fallback. The work notes that determining the truly compatible subset through subset checks would require exponentially many calls. This thesis changes the search variable: instead of choosing $B\subseteq C$ and repeatedly asking whether $B$ is valid, it puts all optional choices in one weighted structure and seeks a maximum-weight valid completion.

The positioning is therefore specific: DINGO and Dang--Ermon inference are exact for regular constraints; Mündler et al. provide an exact CFG completion-feasibility test; and EPIC recovers CFG parallelism through relaxed selection followed by verification. This work does not claim to invent weighted parsing or exact constrained inference in general. Its proposed contribution is to solve the maximum-weight CFG-compatible batch of the current step through one max-plus inference over a finite canvas, while recovering the witness and token provenance.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problema}

\subsection{Token sequences and feasible completions}

Let $\vocab$ be the model vocabulary and $n$ the number of canvas slots. The current state is $x\in(\vocab\cup\{\mask\})^n$. A token completion is $y\in\vocab^n$. We say that $y$ preserves $x$ when
\begin{equation}
 \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad x_i\neq\mask \Rightarrow y_i=x_i.
\end{equation}

Let $\operatorname{detok}:\vocab^n\rightarrow\bytealpha^*$ denote detokenization. To support deterministic lexers or more general lexical relations, define
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda\subseteq\bytealpha^*\times\lexalpha^*,
\end{equation}
where $(b,\ell)\in\Lambda$ means that byte sequence $b$ can produce lexeme sequence $\ell$. The CFG $G=(N,\lexalpha,P,S)$ is defined over lexemes. Length, $\eos$, and $\pad$ conventions are represented by a regular predicate $H(y)$.

\begin{definition}[Feasible completion]
The set of feasible completions at state $x$ is
\begin{equation}
\Feas_x=\left\{y\in\vocab^n\;\middle|\;
\begin{array}{l}
y\text{ preserves }x,\ H(y),\text{ and there exists }\ell\in\lexalpha^*\\
\text{such that }(\operatorname{detok}(y),\ell)\in\Lambda
\text{ and }\ell\in L(G)
\end{array}\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

This definition separates levels that must not be conflated: canvas position, model token, detokenized bytes, lexemes, and grammar terminals. The initial token-aligned version is the special case where $\vocab=\lexalpha$, $\operatorname{detok}$ and $\Lambda$ are identities, and $H$ only requires length $n$.

\subsection{Proposals, weights, and compatibility}

The proposal set is treated as an indexed family
\begin{equation}
 C=(c_1,\ldots,c_m),\qquad c_j=(i_j,t_j,w_j),
\end{equation}
with position $i_j$, token $t_j\in\vocab$, and weight $w_j\geq 0$. Indexing permits multiplicities, although the common policy produces one primary proposal per position. Weights may represent transformed confidence, priority, or unit value. If they are marginal probabilities, their sum must be interpreted as preservation utility rather than the joint probability of the sequence.

For a completion $y$, define
\begin{align}
\Match_C^+(y)&=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:y_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\},\\
R_C(y)&=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j\indic[y_{i_j}=t_j].
\end{align}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility]
An index set $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ is compatible if there exists $y\in\Feas_x$ such that $y_{i_j}=t_j$ for every $j\in J$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[MWPC]
The maximum-weight parallel commitment problem is to compute
\begin{equation}
 J^*\in\argmax_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J),
 \qquad W(J)=\sum_{j\in J}w_j.
\label{eq:mwpc}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Explicit search examines up to $2^m$ sets. The problem, however, has additional structure: every compatible set has a completion as a witness, and every completion defines the proposals it matches.

\subsection{Assumptions and scope of the guarantee}

The main equivalence requires $w_j\geq 0$; the APIs reject negative and non-finite weights. Zero-weight proposals do not change the objective and are omitted from certificates, so the canonical batch is $\Match_C^+(y)$; duplicate positive indices remain distinct.

Optimization is conditioned on the represented support. If an implementation includes only the $K$ most probable tokens at each mask, the result is exact on that truncated lattice, not over all of $\vocab^n$. A full-vocabulary guarantee requires every relevant alternative to be represented explicitly or lazily without unsafe pruning.

Finally, MWPC chooses the best batch at the current step. Commitment changes the next forward-pass input and therefore the future distributions. No claim is made that $J^*$ maximizes cumulative reward through the end of denoising.

\section{Reduction to the Highest-Reward Feasible Completion}
\label{sec:reducao}

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental equivalence]
If $w_j\geq 0$ for every $j$, then
\begin{equation}
\max_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J)
=
\max_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y).
\label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}
If $\Feas_x=\varnothing$, both problems are declared infeasible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any $y\in\Feas_x$, the set $\Match_C^+(y)$ is compatible because $y$ itself satisfies every proposal in that set. Since omitted matched proposals have weight zero,
\begin{equation}
\max_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J)
\geq
\max_{y\in\Feas_x}W(\Match_C^+(y))
=
\max_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y).
\end{equation}

Conversely, let $J$ be compatible and define $J^+=\{j\in J:w_j>0\}$. There is a witness $y\in\Feas_x$ that satisfies every proposal in $J$, so $J^+\subseteq\Match_C^+(y)$. Since the removed indices have zero weight,
\begin{equation}
 W(J)=W(J^+)\leq W(\Match_C^+(y))=R_C(y)
 \leq\max_{y'\in\Feas_x}R_C(y').
\end{equation}
The inequality holds for every compatible $J$. Taking the maximum and combining both directions yields equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal-batch recovery]
If $y^*\in\argmax_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y)$, then the canonical batch $J^*=\Match_C^+(y^*)$ solves MWPC.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$J^*$ is compatible because $y^*$ is its witness. Its weight is $R_C(y^*)$, which equals the MWPC optimum by Theorem~1.
\end{proof}

The theorem eliminates the need to represent subsets in the search space. The parser must find one valid completion whose reward is additive by position or arc. Backtracking that completion simultaneously provides a concrete compatibility certificate and the optimal set.

The grammar need not be unambiguous: reward is attached to tokens or arcs and productions are neutral, so derivations of one completion have equal value. Equal scores use stable traversal; no lexicographic secondary objective is implemented or claimed.

\subsection{A worked commitment example}

Let $G$ generate exactly \texttt{ayz} and \texttt{xbc}, and let the three-slot canvas be entirely masked. Consider proposals $(1,\mathtt{a},4)$, $(2,\mathtt{b},3)$, and $(3,\mathtt{c},2)$. Each is individually feasible. The first proposal is incompatible with either of the others, whereas the second and third can coexist. The two completions score 4 and 5, respectively; thus the optimum is \texttt{xbc}, committing the last two proposals. A greedy policy that accepts the highest-weight proposal first retains only weight 4. The completion certifies compatibility; the decoder need not commit every token it contains.

Slot accounting is a separate issue. With one remaining slot whose tokens emit only \texttt{a} or \texttt{b}, the grammar $S\rightarrow\mathtt{a}\mathtt{b}$ is infeasible. An abstract $\Sigma^*$ gap accepts \texttt{ab} and gives a false positive. If the tokenizer also contains a single token emitting \texttt{ab} and that token is represented in the slot, the same grammar becomes feasible. The bound counts token choices, not emitted characters.

\section{Token-Aligned Algorithm}
\label{sec:tokenaligned}

In this first version, each model token is a CFG terminal and the output has exactly $n$ terminals. Assume that $G$ is in Chomsky normal form, with productions $A\rightarrow BC$ and $A\rightarrow a$. Unit productions, empty productions, and special symbols must be removed or handled by a separate closure.

For each position $i$ and terminal $a$, define the lexical reward
\begin{equation}
s_i(a)=
\begin{cases}
\neginf, & x_i\neq\mask\text{ and }a\neq x_i,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j:i_j=i,\ t_j=a}w_j, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:lexscore}
\end{equation}
The empty sum is zero. Thus, tokens incompatible with fixed positions are impossible, while permitted tokens receive exactly the sum of the proposals they satisfy.

Define $D[A,i,j]$ as the highest reward of a string derivable from $A$ that occupies positions $i,\ldots,j-1$. The base cases are
\begin{equation}
D[A,i,i+1]=\max_{A\rightarrow a\in P}s_i(a).
\label{eq:basecky}
\end{equation}
For longer spans,
\begin{equation}
D[A,i,j]=
\max_{A\rightarrow BC\in P}\ \max_{i<k<j}
\left(D[B,i,k]+D[C,k,j]\right).
\label{eq:cky}
\end{equation}
Impossible entries have value $\neginf$. Backpointers store the terminal, production, and split that attain each maximum.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Token-aligned MWPC through max-plus CKY}
\label{alg:cky}
\small
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require CFG $G=(N,\vocab,P,S)$ in CNF, state $x$, proposals $C$
\Ensure optimal batch $J^*$, witness $y^*$, and value $v^*$, or \textsc{Infeasible}
\State Initialize $D[A,i,j]\gets\neginf$ and empty backpointers
\For{$i\gets 1$ to $n$}
  \ForAll{$A\rightarrow a\in P$}
    \If{$s_i(a)>D[A,i,i+1]$}
      \State $D[A,i,i+1]\gets s_i(a)$; record $(\textsc{Terminal},a)$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\For{$\ell\gets 2$ to $n$}
  \For{$i\gets 1$ to $n-\ell+1$}
    \State $j\gets i+\ell$
    \ForAll{$A\rightarrow BC\in P$ and $k\in\{i+1,\ldots,j-1\}$}
      \State $v\gets D[B,i,k]+D[C,k,j]$
      \If{$v>D[A,i,j]$}
        \State $D[A,i,j]\gets v$; record $(B,C,k)$
      \EndIf
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\If{$D[S,1,n+1]=\neginf$} \Return \textsc{Infeasible} \EndIf
\State $y^*\gets\textsc{Backtrack}(S,1,n+1)$
\State $J^*\gets\{j:y^*_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\}$
\State \Return $(J^*,y^*,D[S,1,n+1])$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{theorem}[Correctness of weighted CKY]
For every entry $D[A,i,j]$ computed by Algorithm~\ref{alg:cky}, its value is the maximum of $\sum_{r=i}^{j-1}s_r(y_r)$ over strings $y_i\cdots y_{j-1}$ derivable from $A$. Consequently, the algorithm returns a maximum-reward completion $y^*\in\Feas_x$ and an optimal MWPC batch.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof is by induction on length $j-i$. For length one, Equation~\ref{eq:basecky} examines every terminal production and assigns the correct score. For a longer span, every CNF derivation begins with some production $A\rightarrow BC$ and splits the string at $k$. By the induction hypothesis, both child entries contain the best values for their subspans; adding them yields the best derivation for that production and split. Taking the maximum over all choices covers every possible derivation. The start entry therefore represents the highest-reward feasible completion. Corollary~1 converts that completion into the optimal batch.
\end{proof}

If $|P_2|$ is the number of binary productions, a direct implementation takes $O(|P_2|n^3+|P_1|n)$ time and $O(|N|n^2)$ memory, in addition to backpointers. This version is the reference implementation: its output can be compared with enumeration of all subsets and all strings on small instances.

\section{Generalization to a Finite Tokenizer-Aware Lattice}
\label{sec:lattice}

\subsection{Token lattice and provenance}

A \emph{weighted token lattice} is a layered directed acyclic graph $A_{\mathrm{tok}}=(Q,E,q_0,F,\omega,\pi)$. For a canvas without early EOS, $Q$ contains layers $0,\ldots,n$; every arc from layer $i-1$ to $i$ corresponds to a token permitted in slot $i$. If $x_i$ is fixed, only its arc exists. If it is masked, there is one arc for every token in the represented support $S_i\subseteq\vocab$.

The weight of an arc labeled $a$ in slot $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\omega(i,a)=\sum_{j:i_j=i,\ t_j=a}w_j.
\end{equation}
The function $\pi(i,a)$ stores the indices of positive-weight proposals satisfied by that arc. A complete path chooses exactly one token per slot, and the sum of its weights is $R_C(y)$. Provenance recovers the canonical $J^*=\Match_C^+(y^*)$ without inferring it again from detokenized text.

When the model permits early termination, the lattice is composed with a regular constraint that accepts one occurrence of $\eos$ followed only by $\pad$, or it uses final states at different layers under explicit semantics. In either case, the number of consumed token arcs remains tied to the real canvas slots.

\subsection{Composition with detokenization and lexing}

Each token may emit a byte string. Conceptually, compose
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{lex}}=
(A_{\mathrm{tok}}\cap A_H)\circ T_{\mathrm{detok}}\circ T_{\mathrm{lex}},
\label{eq:composition}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{detok}}$ maps tokens to bytes, $T_{\mathrm{lex}}$ maps bytes to lexemes, and $A_H$ is an automaton over model-token IDs implementing EOS/PAD and other regular conditions. The synchronized product $A_{\mathrm{tok}}\cap A_H$ precedes detokenization, so both operands read the same alphabet. Weights and provenance originate on token arcs and are carried through composition. An implementation may compute transitions lazily and share prefixes in a tokenizer trie.

For a programming-language lexer, the relation must reflect its declared policy, including maximal munch and priorities. Accepting every possible segmentation may recognize a lexical sequence that the real lexer would never produce. The implemented production path instead gives the CFG raw-byte terminals after the tokenizer mapping; it does not insert a lexer or claim maximal-munch semantics. The separate reference lexical interface is not used by the live model experiments, and contextual lexers remain an extension.

\subsection{Max-plus parsing over the graph}

Let $Q$ be the state set of the resulting lexical graph. For a CFG in CNF, define
\begin{equation}
D[A,p,q]=\text{maximum weight of a path from $p$ to $q$ derivable from $A$}.
\end{equation}
For each terminal arc $e=(p,a,q)$,
\begin{equation}
D[A,p,q]\gets\max(D[A,p,q],\omega(e))
\quad\text{for every }A\rightarrow a.
\end{equation}
For binary productions,
\begin{equation}
D[A,p,q]=
\max_{A\rightarrow BC}\ \max_{r\in Q}
\left(D[B,p,r]+D[C,r,q]\right).
\label{eq:graphdp}
\end{equation}
The algorithm is the Viterbi version of CFG--automaton intersection. A weighted Earley implementation may avoid CNF and exploit top-down guidance, but it must preserve the same maximum and backpointer semantics.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Max-plus parsing over a finite lattice}
\label{alg:graph}
\small
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require strict CNF $G$ with flag $\nu=[\varepsilon\in L(G)]$; finite weighted DAG $A$
\Ensure status, best original path, provenance, and score
\State $(\bar A,\rho,z_\varepsilon)\gets\Call{NormalizeEpsilon}{A}$ \Comment{see text}
\State Index states $q_0,\ldots,q_{m-1}$ in topological order
\State Initialize $D[B,p,q]\gets\neginf$ for $B\in N$ and $p,q\in Q$
\ForAll{arcs $e=(p,a,q)\in\bar E$ and productions $B\rightarrow a$}
  \State relax $D[B,p,q]$ with $\omega(e)$ and record $e$
\EndFor
\For{$\ell=1,\ldots,m-1$}
  \For{$i=0,\ldots,m-1-\ell$}
    \State $j\gets i+\ell$
    \ForAll{$B\rightarrow CD\in P_2$ and $i<k<j$}
      \State relax $D[B,q_i,q_j]$ with $D[C,q_i,q_k]+D[D,q_k,q_j]$
      \State record $(C,D,q_k)$ whenever the score improves
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State $z\gets$ best reconstructed $D[S,q_{\mathrm{start}},f]$ over $f\in F$, or none
\If{$z$ exists} \State expand its edges through $\rho$ \EndIf
\If{$\nu$ and $z_\varepsilon$ exists} \State compare $z$ with $z_\varepsilon$ by original weight \EndIf
\If{neither candidate exists} \State \Return \textsc{InfeasibleOnSupport} \EndIf
\State Recover tokens and all matched positive proposal IDs from the winning original path
\State \Return \textsc{Optimal}, witness, proposal IDs, score, support specification
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Here strict CNF contains only terminal and binary productions; a separate flag records empty-word acceptance. Grammar normalization removes nullable and unit productions before parsing. \textsc{NormalizeEpsilon} computes maximum-weight epsilon-only paths between state pairs in the DAG. For each terminal arc, it creates saturated arcs consisting of an epsilon prefix, that terminal, and an epsilon suffix. Each saturated arc retains its original edge sequence in $\rho$; $z_\varepsilon$ is the best entirely epsilon path from the start to any final state, including a zero-edge path when permitted. Comparing it separately is necessary when a canvas emits only EOS/PAD.

Any original nonempty path can be partitioned around its terminal edges; replacing an epsilon segment by the maximum between the same endpoints cannot reduce its score. Conversely, concatenated saturated arcs expand to an existing original DAG path with the same weight. Equal-score alternatives may share a representative because the objective requires one optimal witness, not every optimum. Original edge IDs preserve token provenance. A completed search alone can return \textsc{Optimal} or \textsc{InfeasibleOnSupport}; a deadline interruption returns \textsc{Timeout} without a certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Lattice soundness, completeness, and optimality]
Assume that: (i) the lattice represents exactly the token sequences permitted by the state and $H$; (ii) lexical composition represents exactly $\Lambda$; (iii) grammar normalization, epsilon saturation, and the separate empty-output candidate preserve maximum scores and reconstructible original paths; and (iv) the span dynamic program completes without unsafe pruning. Then Algorithm~\ref{alg:graph}: (a) returns only a completion in $\Feas_x$; (b) finds a completion whenever $\Feas_x$ is nonempty on the represented support; and (c) returns a maximum-weight MWPC batch on that support.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every reconstructed original path starts at $q_0$, ends at a final state, and follows existing arcs. By assumption (i), it corresponds to a permitted token sequence; by assumption (ii) and the derivation from $S$, its lexical output belongs to $L(G)$. This proves soundness. For completeness, every feasible completion corresponds to a lattice path and some CFG derivation, recovered by epsilon normalization and the terminal/binary rules; entirely epsilon paths are covered by the separate candidate. For optimality, the induction used in Theorem~2 generalizes spans to state pairs: each entry contains the maximum over all compatible paths and derivations. Comparing the best start entry with the empty-output candidate yields $\max_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y)$, and Theorem~1 yields MWPC.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-slot exactness]
If every complete path through $A_{\mathrm{tok}}$ traverses exactly one token arc per permitted slot and composition neither creates nor removes token arcs, every returned witness is representable in the actual number of canvas positions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the layered construction, a start-to-finish path chooses one arc from layer $i-1$ to $i$ for each slot $i$. Internal detokenization or lexing transitions may emit zero or more symbols, but remain associated with the same token macro-arcs. Thus, no accepted path uses more slots than exist or depends on an abstract unbounded region.
\end{proof}

For an explicit epsilon-free graph with $q=|Q|$, the span parser takes $O(|P_2|q^3+|P_1||E|)$ time and $O(|N|q^2)$ chart memory. This excludes grammar normalization and epsilon saturation; $|E|$ counts saturated edges, which may greatly outnumber the original arcs. A naive token lattice may have $O(n|\vocab|)$ arcs. Polynomial complexity therefore does not imply low cost. With top-$K$ support, optimality holds only over the represented alternatives $S_i$.

\section{Iterative Decoder and Progress Guarantee}
\label{sec:decoder}

The optimizer solves one step, but the decoder must define proposals, budget, ties, fallback, and termination. The implemented schedule restricts $C$ to at most $k_s$ eligible positions before MWPC, with one primary proposal per position; a cardinality dimension inside the parser remains an unimplemented extension.

An important case is $J^*=\varnothing$ even though a valid completion exists. The decoder cannot stall. Witness $y^*$ provides a safe progress fallback: committing its token at one masked position preserves that witness as feasible. This token may not be the model's primary proposal, but it guarantees progress without violating the grammar.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Exact parallel-commitment step}
\label{alg:step}
\small
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require model $M$, state $x$, CFG $G$, budget $k_s$, configuration $\Theta$
\Ensure new state $x'$ with completion feasibility preserved, or an infeasibility diagnostic
\State $P\gets M(x)$ \Comment{step logits/distributions}
\State $C\gets\textsc{GenerateProposals}(P,x,k_s,\Theta)$
\State $A\gets\textsc{BuildFiniteLattice}(x,C,\Theta)$
\State $(s_{\mathrm{solve}},J^*,y^*,v^*)\gets\textsc{ParseMaxPlus}(G,A)$
\If{$s_{\mathrm{solve}}\neq\texttt{OPTIMAL}$}
  \State retry wider support only after \texttt{INFEASIBLE\_ON\_SUPPORT}; otherwise retain $s_{\mathrm{solve}}$ and apply the configured failure policy
\ElsIf{$J^*\neq\varnothing$}
  \State commit in $x$ the positions of proposals in $J^*$
\Else
  \State choose eligible masked $i$ maximizing the model probability of $y^*_i$; break ties by lowest absolute position
  \State commit $y^*_i$ at $x_i$
\EndIf
\State \Return $x$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Completion-feasibility invariant]
If $x$ has a feasible witness and a step commits only tokens matching a witness returned by the parser, then the new state still has a feasible witness.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The witness used at the step already preserves every fixed position in $x$. Since the new commitments match it, the witness also preserves the new state and continues to satisfy the lexical, grammatical, and slot conditions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Termination]
Assume that the canvas has finitely many masks, remasking is disabled, every optimizer call returns a feasible witness, and every step commits at least one position. If no earlier step-budget stop is imposed, the decoder completes after at most the initial number of masks, and the final sequence belongs to the constrained language.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The number of masks strictly decreases and cannot be negative. At the final state, the witness that maintains the invariant must match every fixed token, so the output itself is valid.
\end{proof}

\section{Implementation and Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:metodologia}

The local submission release is \texttt{v0.2.0}; its source, evidence and reproduction instructions are delivered together (\path{docs/releases/v0.2.0.md}). Each measurement retains its own clean producing commit. The read-only EPIC submodule remains pinned to \texttt{5b1b310}. Python provides independent reference solvers, proposal/support/lattice construction, validation and the LLaDA adapter. The independent Rust crate \texttt{mwpc\_parser} implements production weighted parsing and reconstruction; \texttt{mwpc\_parser\_py} is a thin PyO3 boundary. The integration preserves \texttt{serial}, \texttt{epic} and \texttt{exact}. Parent software and research artifacts use MIT; manuscript rights remain separate, and upstream notices are retained.

The controlled Python normalizer computes nullable nonterminals, records start-symbol empty acceptance, removes $\varepsilon$ and unit productions, introduces terminal proxies, and right-binarizes longer rules while retaining source-production IDs. The parser boundary accepts only strict terminal/binary CNF plus that empty-string flag. Explicit graph $\varepsilon$-edges are normalized before parsing by maximum-weight acyclic closure; a returned path is expanded to original edge IDs. Thus $\varepsilon$ consumes no physical slot, and neither the Python nor Rust parser performs an implicit cyclic closure. The live structured tasks supply literal UTF-8 byte CFGs directly, so the production path uses no lexical transducer.

For the pinned LLaDA tokenizer, every ordinary base-token piece is mapped compositionally to raw bytes by reversing the GPT-2 ByteLevel bijection; singleton calls to \texttt{decode} are not used to construct lattice emissions. The 126,080 base IDs are ordinary byte-emitting tokens, whereas all 269 added IDs are unsupported as content. The live adapter ranks the maximum needed support once on-device by logit descending and token ID ascending. Each masked row represents its current top-$K$ prefix, the frozen proposal token, and required termination/PAD IDs; fixed rows are singletons. Historical Q5 used $K=8$ and feasibility-driven doubling to 16 and 32, stopping at the first feasible support. Every optimum is therefore reported as \texttt{exact\_on\_support} for the current finite canvas and step, never as a full-vocabulary or future-trajectory optimum.

At each step, the baseline schedule ranks eligible masked positions by primary-token confidence descending and absolute position ascending, retains at most its budget, and creates one frozen primary proposal per position. The live studies use the model softmax confidence itself as the non-negative proposal weight; non-proposal alternatives have zero reward. Equal MWPC scores use stable implementation traversal only---there is no claimed secondary objective. The required EOS profile accepts IDs 126081 and 126348 as the first termination event and uses 126081 as canonical PAD afterward; all specials consume physical slots but emit no CFG byte. The historical adaptive Rust deadline was 30 seconds total, with remaining time passed to each attempt, and \texttt{TIMEOUT} remains distinct from support infeasibility. An optimal zero-match witness advances at the eligible position whose witness token has highest model probability. The reported Q5 exact configuration has no non-optimal fallback; the generic adapter can invoke an explicitly configured serial or EPIC callback while retaining the original solver status and without assigning an exact guarantee to that update.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitacoes}



All optimality claims are per-step \texttt{exact\_on\_support}, over finite represented alternatives. Q5's top-$K$ omits most vocabulary items, possibly changing feasibility or objective; it is not full-vocabulary exact and does not optimize the future trajectory, joint probability, or later logits. At a configured deadline, \texttt{TIMEOUT} leaves feasibility and optimality unknown and carries no certificate, unlike completed \texttt{INFEASIBLE\_ON\_SUPPORT} for the recorded lattice; larger lattices may exhaust time or memory.

The tokenizer contract is the pinned raw ByteLevel byte stream, not singleton-\texttt{decode} Unicode. Added controls are content-unsupported except EOS/PAD; the live path has no programming-language lexer semantics (maximal munch, priority, or context) in the optimizer. Separate task checkers assess functional output. A CFG constrains syntax only, not types, references, execution, or tests. Independent checks mitigate error, but evidence covers one quantized checkpoint and one host. The pilot and confirmation have only twelve tasks each; repetitions do not enlarge those samples. Most nonliteral outputs fail the functional checks. This limits comparisons of practical usefulness. The historical literal-task runtime ratios additionally confound the selection rule with unequal proposal budgets.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusao}

For non-negative weights, the highest-reward representable CFG completion identifies an optimal per-step batch and witness. The finite parser preserves grammar, fixed positions, and slots. The experiments support oracle agreement and expose greedy losses on some synthetic recursive supports. Real replay found no gap, and the matched-budget nonliteral study did not establish a completion, functional-quality or runtime advantage. Future work should reduce repeated lattice construction and parsing costs, evaluate larger competitive proposal sets under matched schedules, and test richer workloads. Such changes require new configurations and measurements rather than extrapolation from the current small study.
