\begin{abstract}
We solve joint selection of a grammar-valid completion and a parallel token batch under a physical-position budget in diffusion language models. A resource-indexed max-plus algorithm computes the exact reward frontier on finite token support, preserving bytes, fixed slots and EOS/PAD. Upper-potential certificates independently prove optimality, rather than merely witness feasibility. We prove universal reward dominance over any feasible batch on the same input and budget, and infinite-family impossibility results: confidence preselection and post-filtering an unbudgeted optimum can lose an arbitrarily large fraction of achievable reward, even for one fixed regular language. The rational reference implementation and exhaustive checks realize the construction. Historical LLaDA/EPIC experiments are secondary, including failed external transfer. The contribution is an incremental mathematical formulation, certifying solution and separation results; it does not assert semantic accuracy, faster execution or future-trajectory optimality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Masked diffusion language models estimate several unknown positions in one forward pass \cite{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada}. Parallel proposals can nevertheless conflict: individually acceptable tokens may jointly eliminate every completion of a JSON document or program. The decoder must select a compatible batch while preserving already committed positions and the finite canvas.

A physical reveal budget makes the decision more subtle: maximizing agreement first and filtering afterwards need not produce the best budgeted batch. We formulate joint completion and commitment, derive a resource-DAG algorithm for every budget, and give independently checkable upper-bound certificates. Theorems establish same-input reward dominance and arbitrarily poor approximation by preselection or post-filtering. Their relevance follows from proofs, independently of benchmark prevalence. Existing finite-slot MWPC supplies the foundation; real dLLM integration and historical negative experiments delimit the application. All guarantees are per step on represented support.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:fundamentacao}

\subsection{Constrained parallel decoding}

A reveal policy proposes tokens from masked-position marginals; serial acceptance preserves feasibility but is order-dependent.

Regular constraints admit exact inference \cite{suresh2025dingo,dang2026automata}; CFG decoding can use completion-language intersection \cite{mundler2025cfg}. EPIC accelerates validation through lexical memoization, Earley parsing and relaxed selection followed by verification \cite{jin2026epic}. Our proposal-agreement objective differs from mean-field MAP; the canonical-catalog MAP control is not a reimplementation of these decoders.

FactorDLM performs exact mean-field inference with finite-domain factor graphs and variable elimination \cite{su2026factor}. Our CFG proposal-agreement solver does not implement that decoder or claim general exact-dLLM-inference novelty.

CLAD performs exact weighted selection on matching conflicts from attention clusters \cite{qi2026clad}, rather than CFG-valid completion. AXON selects supportive reveal anchors using attention and uncertainty \cite{ayoub2026axon}. Exact or capacity-constrained parallel selection is therefore not a general novelty claim here.

Confidence gating and trajectory-based commitment are established \cite{cai2026confidence,wang2026tacg}. Our threshold control around a grammar-certified witness does not reproduce their algorithms or sampling guarantees. Max-marginal filtering predates dLLMs \cite{weiss2010cascades}; our counterfactual margins do not claim to invent it.

\subsection{Weighted grammar--automaton intersection}

CFGs express recursion; CKY and Earley are established parsers \cite{kasami1965,earley1970}. CFG--regular intersection preserves context-freeness \cite{barhillel1961,hopcroft2006}. Weighted CFG optimization \cite{katsirelos2008weighted}, weighted intersection \cite{nederhof2003intersection,hanneforth2011intersection}, and epsilon-preserving constructions \cite{pasti2023intersection} are antecedents. Our finite DAG retains original-path backpointers for one optimal witness.

Semiring parsing separates chart structure from its arithmetic \cite{goodman1999semiring,mohri2002}. Here max-plus uses $\max$ for alternatives, addition for composition, $-\infty$ for impossibility and zero for an empty reward. This maximizes one completion rather than summing probability mass over all CFG words, whose complexity can depend on ambiguity \cite{amarilli2026probabilistic}. Neutral grammar-production weights ensure that multiple derivations do not multiply a token's reward. Certifying dynamic programs also predate this work \cite{demirovic2024certifying}; our contribution specializes resource inference and upper-potential checking to finite token commitment, rather than inventing parsing or proof logging.

\subsection{Tokenization and finite slots}

Subword vocabularies \cite{sennrich2016bpe,kudo2018sentencepiece} do not align with grammar terminals. One model token can emit several bytes or cross a lexical boundary; distinct token sequences can emit identical text. Discarding token provenance would therefore lose both proposal identity and slot accounting. Lexers may additionally impose maximal munch and priorities \cite{aho2006}. We distinguish the general lexical relation from the implemented raw-byte path, which performs no programming-language lexing.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problema}

\subsection{Token sequences and feasible completions}

Let $\vocab$ be the model vocabulary and $n$ the number of canvas slots. The current state is $x\in(\vocab\cup\{\mask\})^n$. A token completion is $y\in\vocab^n$. We say that $y$ preserves $x$ when
\begin{equation}
 \forall i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad x_i\neq\mask \Rightarrow y_i=x_i.
\end{equation}

Let $S_i\subseteq\vocab$ be the declared alternatives in slot $i$, containing its fixed token when committed. For CFG $G=(N,\Sigma,P,S)$, let $\operatorname{emit}(y)$ concatenate token emissions into its terminal alphabet. Production uses compositional bytes; the token-aligned reference uses identity token emissions. A regular predicate $H(y)$ enforces length and EOS/PAD. Optional lexical relations are detailed in the supplement.

\begin{definition}[Feasible completion]
The set of feasible completions at state $x$ is
\begin{equation}
\Feas_x=\left\{y\in\prod_{i=1}^n S_i\;\middle|\;
y\text{ preserves }x,\ H(y),\ \operatorname{emit}(y)\in L(G)\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Positions, model tokens and emitted terminals remain distinct, even when different token sequences emit the same bytes.

\subsection{Proposals, weights, and compatibility}

The proposal set is treated as an indexed family
\begin{equation}
 C=(c_1,\ldots,c_m),\qquad c_j=(i_j,t_j,w_j),
\end{equation}
with position $i_j$, token $t_j\in\vocab$, and weight $w_j\geq 0$. Indexing permits multiplicities, although the common policy produces one primary proposal per position. Weights may represent transformed confidence, priority, or unit value. If they are marginal probabilities, their sum must be interpreted as preservation utility rather than the joint probability of the sequence.

For a completion $y$, define
\begin{align}
\Match_C^+(y)&=\{j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:y_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\},\\
R_C(y)&=\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_j\indic[y_{i_j}=t_j].
\end{align}

\begin{definition}[Compatibility]
An index set $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}$ is compatible if there exists $y\in\Feas_x$ such that $y_{i_j}=t_j$ for every $j\in J$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[MWPC]
The maximum-weight parallel commitment problem is to compute
\begin{equation}
 J^*\in\argmax_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J),
 \qquad W(J)=\sum_{j\in J}w_j.
\label{eq:mwpc}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Explicit search examines up to $2^m$ sets. The problem, however, has additional structure: every compatible set has a completion as a witness, and every completion defines the proposals it matches.

\subsection{Assumptions and scope of the guarantee}

Weights must be finite and non-negative. Zero-weight proposals do not change the objective; certificates omit them and retain distinct positive duplicates. Optimization is exact only on represented alternatives: top-$K$ truncation may change feasibility and reward. Commitments also change later model distributions; this per-step guarantee implies neither full-vocabulary nor future-trajectory optimality.

\section{Reduction to the Highest-Reward Feasible Completion}
\label{sec:reducao}

\begin{theorem}[Fundamental equivalence]
If $w_j\geq 0$ for every $j$, then
\begin{equation}
\max_{J\text{ compatible}}W(J)
=
\max_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y).
\label{eq:equiv}
\end{equation}
If $\Feas_x=\varnothing$, both problems are declared infeasible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any $y\in\Feas_x$, $\Match_C^+(y)$ is compatible with witness $y$ and has weight $R_C(y)$, since omitted matched proposals have zero weight. Therefore the left maximum is at least the right maximum. Conversely, a compatible $J$ has a witness $y$. Its positive-weight indices $J^+=\{j\in J:w_j>0\}$ satisfy $J^+\subseteq\Match_C^+(y)$, so non-negativity gives $W(J)=W(J^+)\leq W(\Match_C^+(y))=R_C(y)$. Taking maxima gives the opposite inequality and equality follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal-batch recovery]
If $y^*\in\argmax_{y\in\Feas_x}R_C(y)$, then the canonical batch $J^*=\Match_C^+(y^*)$ solves MWPC.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$J^*$ is compatible because $y^*$ is its witness. Its weight is $R_C(y^*)$, which equals the MWPC optimum by Theorem~1.
\end{proof}

The theorem eliminates the need to represent subsets in the search space. The parser must find one valid completion whose reward is additive by position or arc. Backtracking that completion simultaneously provides a concrete compatibility certificate and the optimal set.

The grammar need not be unambiguous: reward is attached to tokens or arcs and productions are neutral, so derivations of one completion have equal value. Equal scores use stable traversal; no lexicographic secondary objective is implemented or claimed.

\section{Generalization to a Finite Tokenizer-Aware Lattice}
\label{sec:lattice}

\subsection{Token lattice and provenance}

A \emph{weighted token lattice} is a layered directed acyclic graph $A_{\mathrm{tok}}=(Q,E,q_0,F,\omega,\pi)$. For a canvas without early EOS, $Q$ contains layers $0,\ldots,n$; every arc from layer $i-1$ to $i$ corresponds to a token permitted in slot $i$. If $x_i$ is fixed, only its arc exists. If it is masked, there is one arc for every token in the represented support $S_i\subseteq\vocab$.

The weight of an arc labeled $a$ in slot $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\omega(i,a)=\sum_{j:i_j=i,\ t_j=a}w_j.
\end{equation}
The function $\pi(i,a)$ stores the indices of positive-weight proposals satisfied by that arc. A complete path chooses exactly one token per slot, and the sum of its weights is $R_C(y)$. Provenance recovers the canonical $J^*=\Match_C^+(y^*)$ without inferring it again from detokenized text.

When the model permits early termination, the lattice is composed with a regular constraint that accepts one occurrence of $\eos$ followed only by $\pad$, or it uses final states at different layers under explicit semantics. In either case, the number of consumed token arcs remains tied to the real canvas slots.

\subsection{Composition with detokenization and lexing}

EOS/PAD conditions apply to token IDs before detokenization. Expanding each token arc to bytes preserves its weight and provenance. The implemented CFG consumes raw-byte terminals; it does not insert a lexer or claim maximal-munch semantics. Optional composition with a lexical transducer must implement its declared priorities rather than accept arbitrary segmentation. That reference interface and its composition equation are detailed in the supplement and remain unused in live experiments.

\subsection{Parsing and witness recovery}

The ordinary solver computes a maximum-reward CFG path by max-plus parsing and reconstructs original token arcs. Epsilon closure and strict-CNF normalization retain original-path backpointers, with an explicit empty-word candidate. Terminal/binary span inference is the unbudgeted case of the resource construction proved in Section~\ref{sec:budgeted}. Its token-aligned CKY proof, complete graph algorithm and original soundness/completeness proof are preserved in \path{paper/supplement-foundations.tex}.

Backtracking yields witness $y^*$ and the canonical ordinary MWPC set
\[
J^*\gets\{j:y^*_{i_j}=t_j,\ w_j>0\}.
\]
A completed search alone returns \textsc{Optimal} or \textsc{InfeasibleOnSupport}; a deadline interruption returns \textsc{Timeout} without a certificate.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-slot exactness]
If every complete path through $A_{\mathrm{tok}}$ traverses exactly one token arc per permitted slot and composition neither creates nor removes token arcs, every returned witness is representable in the actual number of canvas positions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the layered construction, a start-to-finish path chooses one arc from layer $i-1$ to $i$ for each slot $i$. Internal detokenization or lexing transitions may emit zero or more symbols, but remain associated with the same token macro-arcs. Thus, no accepted path uses more slots than exist or depends on an abstract unbounded region.
\end{proof}

For an explicit epsilon-free graph, ordinary parsing takes $O(|P_2|q^3+|P_1||E|)$ operations and $O(|N|q^2)$ entries, excluding normalization and saturation. Polynomial complexity does not imply low practical cost. Top-$K$ optimality is restricted to the represented alternatives.

\section{Iterative Decoder and Progress Guarantee}
\label{sec:decoder}

The optimizer solves one step, but the decoder must define proposals, budget, ties, fallback, and termination. In the policy study, its complete selected set remains in the certificate while a separate gate commits a subset. A confidence threshold applies to raw full-vocabulary model probabilities; it is not a calibrated probability of semantic correctness. Committing fewer witness-matching tokens preserves feasibility but changes the future model trajectory. Historical live policies restrict $C$ to at most $k_s$ eligible positions before ordinary MWPC. The new rational reference instead retains all original proposals and optimizes physical commitments inside the parser (Section~\ref{sec:budgeted}); it is not retroactively used in those measurements.

An important case is $J^*=\varnothing$ even though a valid completion exists. The decoder cannot stall. Witness $y^*$ provides a safe progress fallback: committing its token at one masked position preserves that witness as feasible. This token may not be the model's primary proposal, but it guarantees progress without violating the grammar.



\begin{proposition}[Completion-feasibility invariant]
If $x$ has a feasible witness and a step commits only tokens matching a witness returned by the parser, then the new state still has a feasible witness.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The witness used at the step already preserves every fixed position in $x$. Since the new commitments match it, the witness also preserves the new state and continues to satisfy the lexical, grammatical, and slot conditions.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Termination]
Assume that the canvas has finitely many masks, remasking is disabled, every optimizer call returns a feasible witness, and every step commits at least one position. If no earlier step-budget stop is imposed, the decoder completes after at most the initial number of masks, and the final sequence belongs to the constrained language.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The number of masks strictly decreases and cannot be negative. At the final state, the witness that maintains the invariant must match every fixed token, so the output itself is valid.
\end{proof}

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitacoes}



All optimality claims are per-step \texttt{exact\_on\_support}, over finite represented alternatives. Q5's top-$K$ omits most vocabulary items, possibly changing feasibility or objective; it is not full-vocabulary exact and does not optimize the future trajectory, joint probability, or later logits. At a configured deadline, \texttt{TIMEOUT} leaves feasibility and optimality unknown and carries no certificate, unlike completed \texttt{INFEASIBLE\_ON\_SUPPORT} for the recorded lattice; larger lattices may exhaust time or memory.

The budgeted solver is a rational reference, not the Rust production decoder. Its certificate proves optimality of provided weights; semantic relevance of those weights is an application choice. The universal comparison requires identical proposals, support and budget, and proves neither faster parsing nor better answers. Written proofs have not been mechanized in a proof assistant.

The tokenizer contract is pinned ByteLevel bytes, not singleton-\texttt{decode} Unicode; added controls are unsupported except EOS/PAD. No programming-language lexer is integrated into MWPC. A CFG cannot guarantee factual or functional correctness. The historical scoped end-to-end benefit uses synthetic templates, finite catalogs, one quantized checkpoint and one host. A question-derived support can omit the right argument. Free scalar BFCL cases broaden the test but do not constitute the full benchmark; function-name disjointness is weaker than domain disjointness. The measured finite languages are regular, so they do not demonstrate an expressivity advantage for CFGs.

Strict output grading is sensitive to EPIC's lexical layout, hence the separate normalization analysis. EPIC's official recovery can select different completions between processes despite a fixed seed. Repetitions do not enlarge the independent sample; secondary comparisons are exploratory. EOS/PAD rewards may inflate content-relevant scores. Greedy rebuilds charts for unmatched proposals; incremental Boolean parsing is untested. Peak GPU allocation includes weights and CPU peak memory is not measured in the live policy study. Repair remains structural substitution over a restricted schema, without general insertions/deletions or naturally observed model errors.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusao}

Joint budgeted commitment has an exact resource-DAG solution with independently checkable optimality. For the same finite input and budget, no feasible proposal batch has larger reward; confidence preselection and post-filtering an unbudgeted optimum can be arbitrarily worse even under one fixed regular grammar. The budget frontier gives minimum capacity for a reward target, and witness filling attains the update-round lower bound under explicit preservation premises. These mathematical results supply the contribution independently of benchmarks. Weighted parsing, resource dynamic programming and certifying algorithms are established foundations. Historical failed external transfer remains visible. Production Rust support for the new budget dimension, mechanized proof and semantic policy design remain future work; none is required for the stated rational-reference theorems.
