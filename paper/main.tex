\begin{abstract}
A diffusion language model predicts several missing tokens simultaneously.
Conditioning its frozen product prediction on recursive JSON syntax can require
expensive inference, while truncating nesting without an error bound changes
the distribution silently. We specify a certificate that bounds the probability
of valid completions beyond a chosen depth. A deterministic lexical counter
requires the omitted part to close within the remaining slots; a refinement
retains grammatical syntax through the first overflowing original token.
We prove coverage, normalized total-variation and marginal bounds, and a
fixed-tokenizer family in which closure certifies constant depth whereas a
first-overflow certificate requires linearly increasing depth. The latter
forces exponentially many states only for explicit-stack representations,
not for all CFG inference. The certificate also supports exact confidence
commitments and a strictly improving probability reweighting when its condition
holds. A preregistered full-vocabulary comparison confirms the query criterion
in three of fifteen independently captured external states against six
mandatory controls. All thirty iterative MDLM demonstrations return valid JSON.
This is an incremental, scoped inference contribution; classical weighted
abstraction and envelope sampling are antecedents. Semantic superiority,
publication priority and universal decoder speed are not established.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) estimate many missing positions in
one forward pass \cite{sahoo2024mdlm,nie2025llada}. Structured infilling can
preserve existing fields while generating several dependent fragments of JSON.
A consumer needs jointly valid documents, rather than independently plausible
tokens. Exact inference for a frozen product of token probabilities is a useful
component of such a decoder, but its preparation and token-marginal queries
can be expensive. Bounded-depth inference is cheaper in some states; without
a bound on the excluded valid probability, it has no controlled relation to
full grammar conditioning.

Our question is specific: can the remaining \emph{probability of closing}
recursive structures certify a shallow posterior when the \emph{possibility}
of completing a deep grammatical prefix cannot? We construct a deterministic
closure relaxation over original-token events. It remembers lexical state and
physical nesting height, but forgets delimiter types and selected grammar
requirements. A refinement uses exact syntax through the first overflowing
token and this relaxed closure afterward. Each omitted valid event has one
such frontier, including when a subword token contains multiple delimiters.

The mathematical contribution is a delimited comparison of certificates,
including a fixed-vocabulary JSON family, costs in bits and an application
corollary. The practical contribution is a minimal reference implementing
certified mass, original-token marginals and sampling, with a prospectively
selected independent query comparison and an actual iterative MDLM application.
The normalized error remains meaningful even if valid mass is extremely small.
Intervals can decide a commitment policy exactly; they can also certify a
strict increase in valid proposal probability if changing that proposal is
intended. These operations are different and are not conflated.

This does not introduce weighted parsing, probabilistic storage abstraction,
conditioning identities or rejection sampling. Nor does it prove that dLLMs
are preferable to other models for every structured task. The advantage is
useful when a dLLM already supplies parallel predictions and a full conditional
query is expensive. Compact CFG inference, simpler counters and rejection
remain competent alternatives and sometimes win. Mathematical comparison
families are proof objects, never reported as model benchmarks.

\section{Related Work and Scientific Scope}

Exact constrained inference for unambiguous grammars and Markov sources is
established \cite{rivaud2017sampling}; weighted parsing and intersection supply
the necessary dynamic-programming foundations \cite{goodman1999semiring,
nederhof2003intersection}. General ambiguous grammars do not admit an
unqualified word-probability tractability claim \cite{amarilli2026probabilistic}.
Our general posterior library therefore admits checked LL(1) source grammars.
The closure certificate and its application reference specialize deterministic
JSON syntax; this article does not implement a counter abstraction for every
arbitrary input CFG.

Weighted storage approximation already supports probability bounds
\cite{denkinger2017storage}; regular approximation and one-counter relaxation
also precede this work \cite{mohri2001regular,sakharov2017counter}. Our specific
comparison concerns a closing suffix after a unique original-token overflow,
its normalized certificate and a family separating it from first-overflow
mass. Neither regularizing a grammar nor remembering nesting height alone is
claimed as novel.

OS* already combines computable upper proposals, refinement and exact rejection
sampling, including PCFG/LM intersection \cite{dymetman2012os}. An OS* variant
could use the same envelope and share its benefit. CARS learns invalid children
at visited prefixes in autoregressive constrained sampling \cite{parys2025cars};
our exploratory product-source adaptation is not a native CARS execution.
A supplementary exact-envelope extension has a written comparison against
that specified update, not a lower bound against all adaptive samplers.

Regular exact dLLM inference and factor-graph compilation are further
antecedents \cite{dang2026automata,su2026factor}. EPIC uses completion
compatibility and verification to select parallel tokens \cite{jin2026epic};
it does not supply the same full conditional mass and marginals operation.
We retain the pinned upstream decoder and unfavorable historical complete
comparisons. We claim no new EPIC generation victory. Twister distinguishes
per-step product conditioning from conditioning the native masked-diffusion
trajectory \cite{thimmaiah2026twister}; our trajectory reference is explicitly
the former decoder. A proof against that reference does not remove the latter
bias.

\section{Original-Token Problem and Contract}

Let $n$ be the number of physical canvas slots and $\vocab$ the vocabulary of
original model IDs. A compositional byte map emits each supported ordinary
token; different IDs may emit the same bytes. Fixed positions remain singleton
original IDs. For each free position $i$, rational nonnegative weights
$w_i(t)$ sum to $D_i>0$, giving $p_i(t)=w_i(t)/D_i$. The represented source is
\begin{equation}
 q(y)=\prod_{i=1}^n p_i(y_i),\qquad D=\prod_iD_i.
 \label{eq:product}
\end{equation}
The practical full-vocabulary conversion lifts finite binary probabilities
to exact rational weights and normalizes their sum per row. It is exact for
these rounded predictions, not for real-valued neural softmax. MASK is zeroed;
unsupported ordinary byte emissions retain their original denominator mass
and cannot form valid documents. No top-$K$ or gold-token insertion occurs.
EOS termination is absent in this reference; it consumes exactly $n$ slots.

Let $J$ contain all original-token sequences whose bytes are strict UTF-8 JSON
and preserve fixed positions. Aliases are distinct events of $q$, while
multiple grammar derivations never multiply the mass of one event. Delimiters
inside strings do not change nesting; a token can cross several structural
or lexical boundaries. Define $h(y)$ as the maximum physical container depth
encountered throughout its emission, including within tokens, and
$J_d=\{y\in J:h(y)\le d\}$. Put
\begin{equation}
 L=q(J_d),\quad R=q(J\setminus J_d),\quad Z=L+R,
 \qquad \nu=q(\cdot\mid J_d),\quad\mu=q(\cdot\mid J).
\end{equation}
A successful certified query has $L>0$, produces $\nu$ and bounds its distance
to $\mu$. It does not call $\nu$ exact on $J$. Resource refusal, zero shallow
mass, complete valid mass zero and unsupported input are distinct outcomes.
When $L=0$, refine or evaluate the full grammar; an uncomputed tail is no
certificate of infeasibility.

\section{Closure Certificate and Algorithm}

\subsection{Deterministic relaxations}

The lexer deterministically processes bytes, validates UTF-8 and literals,
and emits structural terminals. For each original token and incoming lexical
state, calculate its resulting state and the net, minimum and maximum prefix
height of its emitted structural terminals. These effects include every byte;
looking only at the token's final height can miss an overflow.

The closed counter language $C_d$ requires complete lexing, nonnegative height
at every prefix, final height zero and a peak above $d$. It ignores delimiter
types and key/comma/value grammar rules. Every deep valid JSON event is in
$C_d$, so $U_C=q(C_d)\ge R$. A first-hit bound stops after a complete token
that first exceeds $d$ and treats its remaining probability as one. A stronger
suffix-hit control first checks lexical coaccessibility and safe closing
capacity. These are necessary tests, not the probability of closing.

For the grammar-hit language $G_d$, retain the deterministic grammatical
prefix through the \emph{end} of its first overflowing token; the remaining
tokens are free. For the handoff language $B_d$, require that same prefix
and then the lexical, nonnegative, zero-ending counter suffix. The admitted
necessary suffix tests must be safe for every valid completion. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 J\setminus J_d\subseteq B_d\subseteq G_d,
 \qquad B_d\subseteq C_d,
 \qquad R\le U_B\le\min(U_G,U_C).
 \label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
For a fixed $L>0$, $U/(L+U)$ increases with $U$; handoff is at least as strong
a certificate as both controls \emph{at the same depth}. This dominance does
not establish a universal runtime advantage.

\subsection{Integer recurrence and correctness}

Bounded JSON inference keeps exact lexical and grammar-stack states while
height is at most $d$. Let $\alpha_{i,a}$ be the integer sum of prefixes
occupying $i$ slots and reaching state $a$ without overflow. Preserve this
prefix graph before trimming to accepting paths in $J_d$: prefixes lacking
a shallow conclusion may still have deep valid completions. For a first-overflow
edge $g$ at slot $i$, let $W_{i,g}$ sum its original token weights, with final
lexer state $s_g$ and height $k_g$. Let $\beta_{i+1,s_g,k_g}$ sum counter-valid
suffix weights. Then
\begin{equation}
 DU_B=\sum_{i,a,g\text{ first overflow}}
        \alpha_{i,a}W_{i,g}\beta_{i+1,s_g,k_g}.
 \label{eq:handoff}
\end{equation}
Replacing $\beta$ by $\prod_{j>i}D_j$ gives $DU_G$. Standard finite-state
inside/outside computes $DL$ and original-token marginal numerators in $J_d$.
Exact integer random choices reconstruct original IDs, not only token classes.
Sampling-law proofs assume a uniform integer random oracle; seeded pseudorandom
executions are reproducible functional traces, not proofs of the entropy source.

\begin{proposition}[Original-event coverage]
Equation~\eqref{eq:handoff} is the mass of $B_d$ without event multiplicity.
It bounds the omitted valid mass, including aliases and multi-delimiter tokens.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A deep valid word has exactly one first overflowing original token. Its
complete prefix is grammatical and its remaining suffix is lexically complete,
nonnegative and closes its height. Thus it contributes to an admissible term.
All accepted handoff words have the unique same decomposition. Grouping sums
weights of distinct original IDs having identical deterministic effects; it
does not assign multiple derivations to one ID word. Prefix and suffix occupy
disjoint slots, so multiplying their weights is valid under~\eqref{eq:product}.
Division by $D$ gives the claimed mass and the inclusions in~\eqref{eq:coverage}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Normalized posterior certificate]
If $L>0$ and $U\ge R$, then
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{TV}(\nu,\mu)=\frac R{L+R}
       \le\delta:=\frac U{L+U}.
 \label{eq:tv}
\end{equation}
For any event $A$, including an original-token coordinate,
\begin{equation}
 (1-\delta)\nu(A)\le\mu(A)\le(1-\delta)\nu(A)+\delta.
 \label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On $J_d$, $\mu=(L/Z)\nu$; its remaining mass is $R/Z$. The positive and
negative parts of $\nu-\mu$ each sum to $R/Z$, proving the identity. Monotonicity
in $R$ gives the bound. Write $\mu=(L/Z)\nu+(R/Z)\rho$ on the deep part and
use $0\le\rho(A)\le1$ and $R/Z\le\delta$ to obtain both interval endpoints.
\end{proof}

The algorithm tries a declared increasing depth grid. It computes $L$ and
$U_G$, accepts if normalized error is small enough, otherwise optionally
computes $U_B$ before increasing depth. The closed alternative computes $U_C$.
All failed trials are charged. A final unrestricted exact query is allowed;
there is no uncertified success fallback. This is an approximation of the
\emph{posterior}, with exact mass and sampling within $J_d$.

\begin{proposition}[Strict refinement over both component bounds]
The ratio $\min(U_G,U_C)/U_B$ can be arbitrarily large at the same depth,
even on a two-slot JSON token source.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At depth one, let the first slot emit \verb|[[|, \verb|[[,]]| or \verb|[]|
with probabilities $s,t,1-s-t$, where $s,t>0$ and $s+t<1$. The second
emits \verb|]]| with probability $0<r<1$, otherwise whitespace. Direct
partition of original events gives
\[
 L=(1-s-t)(1-r),\quad R=sr,\quad U_G=s,\quad
 U_C=sr+t(1-r),\quad U_B=sr.
\]
The counter admits the balanced but invalid comma word; grammar-hit admits
a completable opening prefix without paying for its rare closing. Handoff
removes both excesses. With $s,t$ fixed and $r\to0$, the ratio diverges.
\end{proof}
This is an illustration of a strict bound, not a favorable model benchmark
or a runtime separation: exact inference in this tiny example is also cheap.
Intersection of upper languages is classical; the implementation uses its
unique frontier to avoid retaining full deep typed stacks.

\subsection{Cost, including preparation and precision}

Let $Q$ count lexical states, $F$ the total counter effects, $H_d$ the bounded
prefix-graph size and $D_{\max}$ the physical maximum possible height from all
token emissions. A counter forward pass uses $O(nD_{\max}F)$ arithmetic
operations and $O(QD_{\max})$ integers with two layers. Handoff caches suffix
states $(i,s,k)$, up to $O(nQD_{\max})$ cells, and evaluates its frontier in
$O(H_d)$ additional graph operations plus effects of overflow tokens. A bounded
stack can contain $O(c\sum_{k=0}^d|\Gamma|^k)$ states for control size $c$ and
frame alphabet $\Gamma$; no polynomial claim is made for growing $d$.

Token bytes and lexer-table preparation are paid, as are full-vocabulary
weight conversion and all depth trials. Emitting all original marginals costs
at least $\Omega(f|\vocab|+n)$ for $f$ free positions; sampling explicitly writes
$n$ IDs. Integer submasses have $O(\sum_i\operatorname{bitlen}D_i)$ bits, apart
from bounded additions and representation overhead; operation counts must
be multiplied by the appropriate integer-operation cost. The total repeated
cost is preparation plus the sum of complete queries, including model forwards,
updates and output. Low warm query latency never removes expensive preparation.

\section{A Comparative Advantage Independent of Benchmarks}

The following family establishes certificate strength, not observed neural
behavior. Use the fixed tokenizer with byte emissions
\verb|{"payload":|, quote, \verb|[|, \verb|{"k":|, whitespace, \verb|0|,
\verb|]| and \verb|}|. Whitespace in this family is the single ASCII space
byte, including within strings; it is not a tab or newline.
The outer-object prefix and final closing brace are
fixed. In this family $n\ge2$ denotes its index and the physical canvas has
$3n+4$ slots. The first payload token is quote with
probability $3/4$ or array-open with $1/4$. Each of the next $n$ positions has
array-open with $1-1/n$, object-key-open with $1/(2n)$ or whitespace with
$1/(2n)$. The central token is quote or zero, each with $1/2$. Each of the
remaining $2n$ positions has closing bracket with $\phi_j/(4n)$, closing brace
with $(1-\phi_j)/(4n)$, or whitespace with $1-1/(4n)$, where
$1/4\le\phi_j\le3/4$. Types may vary by position.

\begin{theorem}[Constant versus growing certification depth]
For this family, closed counter or handoff at depth one satisfies
\begin{equation}
 L_1\ge\frac3{32},\quad U_1\le\frac1{8(n+1)!},\quad
 \operatorname{TV}(q(\cdot\mid J_1),q(\cdot\mid J))
       \le\frac4{3(n+1)!}.
 \label{eq:separation}
\end{equation}
The grammar-first-overflow certificate is at least $1/9$ at every $d\le n+1$,
even with exact necessary feasibility tests. Thus error $10^{-3}$ is certified
at $d=1$ for $n\ge6$ by closure, whereas grammar-hit must use $d\ge n+2$ or
full inference. Explicit-stack expansion then retains at least $2^n$ distinct
positive, completable type stacks; this is not a lower bound on compact CFG
inference or other abstractions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the quote branch, excluding all object-key tokens has probability at least
$1/2$ by the union bound, closing the string costs $1/2$, and all final tokens
being whitespace has probability at least $1/2$. Hence $L_1\ge3/32$.
A lexical deep completion needs the array branch and central zero, costing
$1/8$. If $W$ counts whitespace in the opening block and $C$ counts final
closings, counter closure requires $W+C=n+1$. The sum of the corresponding
independent Bernoulli probabilities is one. For any such sum $S$,
$\Pr(S\ge k)\le(\sum_jp_j)^k/k!$: union over $k$ successful positions, then
expand the power and count the $k!$ orders of every distinct-index term.
Thus $U_1\le1/[8(n+1)!]$, and $U_1/L_1$ gives the TV bound.

The array branch followed by $n$ opening tokens has probability at least
$(1/4)(1-1/(2n))^n\ge1/8$. Every one of its $2^n$ type stacks can be completed
with zero and $n+1$ matching closings within the $2n$ final slots. Feasibility
trimming therefore retains them. Each prefix reaches depth $n+2$, giving
$U_G\ge1/8$ for $d\le n+1$. Since $L_d\le1$, its normalized bound is at least
$1/9$. At $d\ge n+2$, explicit stacks remain distinct. Closure instead uses
constant bounded stack depth and $O(n^2)$ counter arithmetic; rational inputs
of $O(\log n)$ bits give $O(n\log n)$ submass bits and polynomial bit cost.
\end{proof}

The JSON payload has multiple string and recursively typed alternatives;
the grammar does not encode a particular answer. The dLLM connection is its
parallel product prediction, not a claim that any real checkpoint necessarily
has these weights. Zeros outside declared supports are explicit. Perturbing
row products by total coupling probability at most $\tau$ changes each event
mass by at most $\tau$, giving $L\ge3/32-\tau$ and
$U\le1/[8(n+1)!]+\tau$; a strictly positive-vocabulary version remains certified
when $\tau$ is small enough. This family is not included as a favorable
performance benchmark.

\section{Useful Decisions from the Certificate}

\subsection{Conditional-confidence commitment and a whole decoder}

For a sampled valid completion, the reference policy tests whether its
original-token marginal under $\mu$ is at least $4/5$ at each free position,
commits accepted positions in index order up to four, and otherwise commits
the first free sampled position. If $N_{i,t}=Dq(J_d\cap\{Y_i=t\})$,
$l=DL$ and $u=DU$, Equation~\eqref{eq:interval} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \frac{N_{i,t}}{l+u}\le\mu_i(t)\le\frac{N_{i,t}+u}{l+u}.
\end{equation}
An interval wholly above the threshold proves acceptance; one strictly below
proves rejection. If ambiguous, refine the same frozen query or use full exact
inference, keeping the \emph{same sampled completion}. Threshold equality is
accepted only when certified. No discontinuous approximate threshold heuristic
is substituted. Consequently the commitment map equals the full-reference
map on every admitted sampled word.

\begin{corollary}[Common-policy trajectory bound]
If at most $m$ progressing steps use conditional sampling error at most
$\varepsilon/m$, and every confidence decision resolves, the resulting output
law is within TV $\varepsilon$ of the full frozen-posterior decoder with the
same policy, assuming all preparation and recognition operations conclude.
Every returned output preserves fixed original IDs and is valid JSON.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply a maximal coupling at equal histories. Common commitment maps preserve
equality when samples agree, so the next model inputs and frozen products also
agree. The probability of a first divergence is bounded by the sum of local
errors, at most $\varepsilon$. Deterministic maps contract TV. Each commitment
retains a valid witness and fixes at least one position; after at most $m$
steps, that property certifies the final document.
\end{proof}
This is the classical coupling argument applied to a precisely shared policy,
not a guarantee for interrupted runs or the native conditioned MDLM trajectory.
For any quality score in $[0,1]$, its expected difference between these two
ideal decoders is consequently at most $\varepsilon$, without benchmarking
that score. This preserves the reference's quality up to the budget; it does
not establish that the reference is accurate or better than EPIC.

\subsection{Strictly increasing valid proposal mass}

Changing the proposal is an optional, separate operation. At a free original
coordinate, let $r=p_i(t)$ and $v=\mu_i(t)$. Multiplying its integer weight by
$a>1$ and renormalizing \emph{only that row} produces $q'$, with the identity
\begin{equation}
 \frac{q'(J)}{q(J)}=\frac{1+(a-1)v}{1+(a-1)r}.
 \label{eq:amplification}
\end{equation}
The identity follows by partitioning $J$ on $Y_i=t$. If
$b=N_{i,t}/(l+u)>r$, the ratio is at least
$[1+(a-1)b]/[1+(a-1)r]>1$. Thus the certificate can justify a strict improvement
before sampling; it never amplifies a token merely because a truncated
marginal looks large. Selecting the largest certified gain uses exact integer
comparisons. It preserves support and fixed IDs but explicitly changes the
conditional law. Rejection from $q'$ needs fewer expected proposals, yet paying
for marginal inference may eliminate a total-time advantage. This classical
reweighting corollary neither asserts semantic improvement nor runs inside
the unchanged-product decoder above.

\subsection{Separate exact-envelope extension}

Because $J_d$ and $B_d$ are disjoint and cover $J$, the mixture proportional
to their original masses is $q(\cdot\mid J_d\cup B_d)$. Rejecting only invalid
JSON gives exactly $\mu$ on acceptance. Its success probability is
$Z/(L+U_B)\ge1-\delta$, and $k$ attempts fail with probability at most $\delta^k$.
Finite-budget acceptance is locally exact; the kernel also contains FAIL.
With bounded per-step errors, its full interrupted trajectory has TV at most
$\sum_t\delta_t^k$ against the frozen-posterior reference, not unconditional
equality. Conditioning an entire multi-step run on success can bias earlier
choices. Resource failures are additional and are not covered by this bound.

This follows the OS* envelope principle, not a new generic sampler. The
extension is preserved separately in attempt25 with strong counter+CARS and
compact CFG controls. Its complete development measurements are reported separately;
query speedups below are not exact-envelope-sampling speedups.

\section{Implementation, Verification and Protocol}

The reference retains immutable attempts and prior negative results. General
MWPC maximizes proposal weights, whereas the present query computes a conditional
posterior; production serial, EPIC and exact strategies remain separate.
The JSON reference uses deterministic lexical effects, bounded predictive
stacks, integer inside/outside and original-ID reconstruction. No model tensor
or tokenizer assumption is hidden in a generic optimizer. Full compiler costs
include admission, tokenization and normalization where applicable; exact
compact baselines receive the same syntax coarsening, lexical coaccessibility
and safe suffix checks. Token aliases and byte prefixes remain distinct.

Independent finite product oracles enumerate the accepted events and actual
random-choice law using strict UTF-8/Python JSON recognition. They cover
aliases, fixed IDs, UTF-8 splits, intra-token peaks, empty shallow support,
zero mass and finite-budget refusal. The full-vocabulary oracle checks
1,206,192 original choices in 24 one-hole frames, with 168 exact comparisons.
The focused suite currently contains 87 tests; the historical suite requested
removed remains only in archives. Tests establish implementation evidence,
not novelty or universal physical speed.

Lean checks selected algebraic and count specifications. New files prove
seven finite-budget rejection/count results and two mass-amplification
comparisons, with axiom audits and no admissions. They do not prove the Python
lexer, frontier coverage, normalizer, actual sampler implementation, neural
model or whole trajectory. The general posterior and commitment specifications
remain available, with their narrower contracts.

The full-cost query protocol was frozen before competitive development timings.
Both candidates, closed and handoff, are compared against grammar-hit,
suffix-hit, exact stack, and three compact full CFG kernels: local,
bidirectionally trimmed local, and epsilon-local. These six controls are
mandatory. Raw rejection is separately a first-sample control, since it does
not provide certified full marginals. Every method receives identical original
full-vocabulary predictions, fixed canvas, lexer rules and budgets. Preparation,
weight conversion, every trial, sampling, outside and all original marginals
are charged. Archived synchronized model-forward/full-softmax time is included;
model loading and CUDA warmup are reported separately. Decoder cold startup
includes lexer table or normalizer and is not whole-server cold startup.

The primary gate requires at least 20\% improvement in the median paired total
wall \emph{and} CPU ratios against every mandatory control, with favorable
sign in every completed repetition. A consistent localized internal capacity
refusal can support a budget-scoped gain; timeout never proves infeasibility,
and external-supervisor failures do not receive that credit. All methods have
120 seconds and 3 GiB solver address space, with a 150-second supervisor and
10-million state/edge and 50-million term guards. Rejection receives one million
attempts; its refusals concern that explicit ceiling. Outputs, errors and
refusals are all retained. The observed sample size counts states/documents,
not repeated measurements as independent datasets.

Development uses all 18 previously captured JSON-Schema-Test-Suite states \cite{jsonschematestsuite},
three repetitions. Select only here, tie handoff; zero primary wins rejects
the gate. Independent confirmation uses all five eligible positive files of
JSONTestSuite \cite{nstjsontestsuite} at pinned revision \texttt{1ef36fa}, their unchanged bytes and
4/8/16 uniformly selected masks, nine rotated repetitions. Eligibility is
strict UTF-8/Python JSON and 16--96 GPT-2 tokens; neither grammar nor support
uses the answer to choose a favorable completion. These are external syntax

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

The incremental claim is a particular closing-tail certificate and its
original-token implementation, comparative certificate theorem and useful
conditional decisions. Related work already supplies generic approximation,
weighted inference, envelope refinement and coupling. We have no human review,
confirmed publication priority or promise of acceptance. The supplied proof
can be reviewed without executing code; experiments independently assess
whether its sufficient condition is useful on real predictions.

The practical query gain is conditional: three of fifteen external states
pass a strict prespecified gate, while simple methods remain faster elsewhere.
Handoff has stronger theoretical bounds but did not establish an additional
20\% runtime advantage over closed. Compact CFG algorithms can share the
polynomial benefit in the proof family. Internal capacity refusals are not
universal physical impossibility. Larger documents, other grammars,
tokenizers and checkpoints may lose the advantage; exact fallback can be
expensive. JSON syntax permits duplicate keys and provides no factual,
functional, calibration or semantic-range guarantee.

The actual infilling application supplies conditional-confidence decisions
using new forwards and valid context-preserving outputs. One seed is functional
evidence, not statistical whole-decoder latency confirmation or semantic
quality. Posterior error refers to the frozen-product decoder, not the native
conditioned model joint. Probability amplification changes $q$ intentionally;
its strict mass benefit can be too small to amortize preparation. Finite sampler
budgets and resource interruptions require separate outcomes.

Thus the supported improvement is controlled shallow grammar inference for
parallel model predictions, with a mathematically stronger certificate than
first-hit in the stated family and a confirmed query-cost advantage in a
limited external cohort. Existing exact posterior, MWPC and pinned EPIC remain
available under their own contracts. Broader deployment, independent human
proof review and native complete-decoder comparisons remain open; they are
not represented as completed work.
