# V9 — Real-operation lead audit; no application established yet

2026-10-17. Read-only primary context, before selecting another consumer or
running model/data work. For README1 the existing scalar-loss/full-generation
objections remain; for2--8 the mathematical result/controls/cost obligations
are unchanged. This note does NOT justify a new large implementation.

The original [NRCSE report](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/NRCSE/pdf/trs17_RSS.pdf),
*Ranked Set Sampling for Ecological Research*, Section3.3 and Section3.4
through the reported cost discussion were read. It describes cheap visual
versus expensive physical stream-habitat measurements and a21-stream study.
It explicitly accounts for ranking errors and full sampling cost. Its Taylor
River cost figures refer to a small unpublished preliminary study reported
inside that report, not new A63 observations. No raw stream data were obtained,
no ratios transferred to a model, and no contemporary cost claim is made.

The archived [EPA QA/G-5S](https://archive.epa.gov/nheerl/arm/web/pdf/epaqag5s.pdf)
was opened at its introductory scope/contents, identifying Section8 and
Appendix8-A for ranked-set designs. Those sections were NOT fully read here.
The report is guidance from2002; it is not presented as a current legal
requirement, an endorsed A63 protocol or a validated own application.

This supplies an actual class of two-phase measurement operations to inspect,
but NOT the exact candidate contract. Neither examined material establishes
three intrinsic global proxy categories, a retained literal first sample,
unknown category masses under the supplied information, or a practical reason
to require the exact six-pool pair law. A set of size three is NOT the same
as a source with three allowed keys. Continuous RSS, multiple-response designs,
two-phase regression and known-map stratification remain mandatory controls.

Therefore do not relabel the field examples as an A63 benefit, create an
artificial three-bin/two-output benchmark or claim a new field protocol.
The smaller next useful step is to establish an exact consumer information/
output contract, or derive a more general consequence that a real consumer
actually needs. Mathematical callback savings alone are still insufficient
for the full original user goal. No new code/head/score/label/timing here.
