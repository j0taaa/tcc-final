# Erratum: the rare-event comparison in theory-v1 Section7 is wrong

2026-10-11. Preserve `theory-v1.md` and its frozen inventories exactly as
historical evidence. This file corrects its final comparison; it does not
relabel its executions or manufacture a positive decision.

For two outputs BOTH having event probability a, every coupling obeys

    pass@2 <= min(1,2a).

The iid pass@2 is2a-a^2. Therefore the largest possible gain over iid is

    min(a^2,(1-a)^2).

For a=epsilon<=1/2 this is epsilon^2 for EVERY coupling, not just a repair
that activates on collisions. A direct marginal-preserving antithetic pair
CANNOT improve coverage by epsilon(1-epsilon). At epsilon=.01 the iid
coverage is.0199 and the maximum is.02: gain.0001, not.0099. The stated
99-fold advantage in the historical final paragraph is invalid.

The collision-family bound min(d(E),D-d(E)) remains correct. The distinction
is that on a two-point rare-event distribution it matches the global bound.
Indeed, its one-proposal MH version gains (1-epsilon)*epsilon^2, attaining
1-epsilon of the best possible gain; e.g.99% when epsilon=.01. A perfect
stationary collision repair can attain the global bound when its transition
probabilities are available. Thus rarity alone does NOT distinguish collision
repair from other exact marginal-preserving pairs by that alleged factor.

The earlier conclusion must not be justified by this erroneous comparison.
Other objections remain: small absolute pair gain, known MH/Dirichlet
principles, broad-support uniform limitation, extra sample cost, stronger
controls and unconfirmed priority/application. This correction is not a
confirmation that A29 satisfies the publication goal. The current main
article did not repeat the incorrect numeric claim (search checked on
2026-10-11); its research notebook and A29 README did, as historical records.
