\begin{abstract}
Structured diffusion decoders often condition a frozen product prediction on
a recursive grammar. Comparing two such predictions requires measuring their
conditioned distributions, rather than comparing independent token marginals.
We give an integer implementation of planar support compression with
$O(\eta^{-1/2})$ generators and near-linear construction cost. Combined with
known admissible-gate propagation, this yields deterministic rational relative
total-variation certificates on unambiguous event forests, with polynomial
bit complexity and no positive-mass or positive-distance floor. For ordinary
products the arithmetic bound is
$O(nq\sqrt{n/\varepsilon}\log(q+2))$, improving the precision
exponent of the stated deterministic bounds examined here. A classical chart
construction gives a full-input polynomial corollary for trimmed LL(1)
grammars and original token identities. An audit on one public diffusion-model
head illustrates probability-table compression. All 24 format/tolerance/method
combinations are reported against adaptive angular compression and support-aware
Hellinger, chi-square and likelihood controls. The FP16 certificate is
substantially tighter than the strongest inexpensive bound, while several
alternative implementations are faster. The geometric and inference
principles are antecedents; the contribution is quantitative and concerns
distribution auditing, without claiming faster generation or semantic gains.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and quantitative contribution}
Let $S_1,\ldots,S_n$ be finite domains of original token identities. Each token
has a fixed byte emission. A canvas and an unambiguous grammar specify a
common valid set $\mathcal F\subseteq\prod_i S_i$. For two nonnegative rational
tables $p_i,q_i$ with row sums at most one, let
\[
 Z_p=\sum_{y\in\mathcal F}\prod_i p_i(y_i),\qquad
 P(y)=\frac{\prod_i p_i(y_i)}{Z_p},
\]
and define $Z_q,Q$ analogously. We require $Z_p,Z_q>0$; otherwise this comparison
is undefined. Zeros, token aliases, fixed positions and rare valid events are
allowed. The target is a rational interval
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 L\leq\TV(P,Q)\leq U\leq(1+\varepsilon)L,
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon\leq1.
\end{equation}
This is the frozen, grammar-conditioned product law of a step, not the
learned joint law of a complete neural trajectory. No EOS is admitted here.

Masked diffusion models motivate parallel structured sampling~\cite{mdlm}.
Grammar conditioning and semiring parsing are antecedents~\cite{rivaud,goodman};
this paper audits the distributional effect of numerical tables used in that
operation. EPIC~\cite{epic} instead accelerates grammar-constrained commitment.
These tasks are distinct; computing a tighter drift certificate does not
establish a faster or more accurate EPIC replacement.

TV as a support query, admissible-gate composition and deterministic relative
approximation are established principles~\cite{fu}. Classical convex
approximation gives the planar exponent~\cite{dudley}. Our construction
retains original integer polygon endpoints; its generators have no new
denominators, and its nearest-edge queries use exact integer comparisons.
These properties provide the explicit arithmetic and bit bounds below.
We do not claim a new geometric approximation rate or a TV lower bound.

Feng, Liu and Liu's product FPTAS has stated arithmetic cost~\cite[Theorem 1]{feng}
\[
 O(qn^2\varepsilon^{-1}\log q\log(n/(\varepsilon\TV))).
\]
Its $(1-\varepsilon)$ convention converts to~\eqref{eq:target} by using
error $\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)$. Fu's paired product corollary gives
$\widetilde O(nq(n/\varepsilon)^4)$ and already removes numerical TV dependence
in an extended real-arithmetic model~\cite[Corollary 1.4]{fu}.
Our sharper product bound uses precision exponent $1/2$ and polynomial-bit
integers. Comparisons are of published upper bounds, not lower bounds on
competent alternative implementations or guarantees of physical speed.
Bhattacharyya, Feng and Srivastava~\cite[Sections 2--3]{gaussian} restate the
Feng bound and simplify its extension-distance proof; they already credit
its polynomial bit complexity. Integer computability alone is therefore
not our claimed improvement. Fu already removes the positive-TV dependence.

Randomized inference-based relative approximation is also an antecedent
~\cite{inference}; a derivation encoding supplies a relevant alternative,
as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:related}. Randomization may be preferable
when a probabilistic failure bound is acceptable. The finite numerical audit
does not compare those algorithms' runtimes.

\section{An exact integer planar core}
For a finite multiset $G$ of integer pairs, put
\[
 F_G(w)=\sum_{g\in G}|g\cdot w|,
 \qquad Z(G)=\sum_{g\in G}[-g,g].
\]
Thus $F_G$ is the support function of the centrally symmetric polygon $Z(G)$.

\begin{theorem}[Integer endpoint compression]\label{thm:planar}
Given $m$ integer pairs and integer $k\geq1$, one can construct a multiset
$C$ of at most $64k$ integer pairs such that, for every $w\in\mathbb R^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core}
 (1-(16k^2)^{-1})F_G(w)\leq F_C(w)\leq F_G(w).
\end{equation}
The construction uses $O(m\log(m+2))$ exact arithmetic operations, or
$O(m)$ if the nonzero, noncollinear generators are already in canonical
angular order. The returned core preserves that order.
Each output coordinate is a signed sum of input coordinates. Rank zero or
one is compressed exactly. Intermediate integers have polynomial input bits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Orient nonzero generators into a half-plane and sort by determinant. Starting
at minus their sum and walking by twice each generator, then its opposite,
enumerates $Z(G)$. Every polygon vertex has the same coordinate parities.
Redundant collinear vertices may be removed. For rank two choose a vertex $u$
of maximum squared Euclidean norm and a vertex $v$ of maximum
$|\det(u,v)|$, reversing $v$ to make $D=\det(u,v)>0$. If
$T=[u\ v]^{-1}$ and $K=TZ(G)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalization}
 \operatorname{conv}(\pm e_1,\pm e_2)\subseteq K
 \subseteq[-2,2]\times[-1,1].
\end{equation}
Indeed the coefficient $\beta=\det(u,x)/D$ is at most one in absolute
value. Writing $x=\alpha u+\beta v$, maximality of $\|u\|$ gives
$|\alpha|\|u\|\leq\|x\|+\|v\|\leq2\|u\|$. The inner inclusion follows
from the four chosen vertices. Hence $h_K(w)\geq\|w\|_\infty$.

Take the $32k$ points of spacing $1/k$ on the boundary of $[-4,4]^2$.
For each, find a closest polygon edge and retain both of its endpoints and
their opposites. Let $R$ be their convex hull. Clearly $R\subseteq K$.
For a unit supporting normal $w$, let $x$ maximize $w\cdot x$ on $K$.
The ray $x+tw$ reaches the outer square at $p$, with $t\geq2$.
A grid point $q$ on that side has $\|q-p\|\leq1/(2k)$.
Let $y$ be the projection of $q$ onto $K$. Projection optimality gives
\[
 (q-y)\cdot(x-y)\leq0,\qquad
 t\,w\cdot(x-y)\leq-\|x-y\|^2-(q-p)\cdot(x-y)
 \leq\frac{\|q-p\|^2}{4}.
\]
Because $y$ belongs to a retained edge, it is in $R$. Thus
$h_K(w)-h_R(w)\leq1/(32k^2)$. Since $h_K(w)\geq1/2$ for unit $w$,
homogeneity gives~\eqref{eq:core} for these two polygons.

Map the retained vertices back by $T^{-1}$. Their centered hull has
$2r\leq128k$ vertices $z_0,\ldots,z_{2r-1}$ in boundary order, with
$z_{i+r}=-z_i$. Its generators are
$b_i=(z_{i+1}-z_i)/2$, $0\leq i<r$. They are integers by the common parity,
and each is the sum of a consecutive run of oriented original generators.
Their starting vertex is $-\sum_i b_i=z_0$, so they represent the hull
exactly. One may return the original list if it is shorter.

If $m\leq k$ return the original ordered generators exactly, avoiding a large
grid. Otherwise $k<m$. Polygon construction after sorting costs $O(m)$;
constant-factor normalization uses two linear scans. Lemma~\ref{lem:query}
handles all outside queries together in $O(m+k)=O(m)$ time. Mark retained
vertices and opposites in a Boolean array, then scan in original boundary
order instead of sorting a new hull. Consecutive first-half edge differences
are already cyclically ordered; canonical orientation introduces at most
one wrap. Locate the minimum angle by positive determinant comparison and
rotate the list. This costs $O(m)$ and yields ordered output. The only
$O(m\log(m+2))$ work is the initial sort when the input is unordered.
After scaling by $kD$, all geometry uses integer cofactors, determinants,
squared segment distances and cross multiplication. No projection coordinate
is output and no new rational denominator is introduced.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Monotone outside sweeps]\label{lem:query}
For a convex polygon strictly to the left of the outside query line, all
queries on that line in increasing vertical order have exact nearest edges
in linear total arithmetic time in the polygon and query counts.
Quarter-turns handle the other three sides.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The projection lies on the right boundary chain, from the rightmost
lowest vertex to the rightmost highest vertex. Along this chain the second
coordinate increases strictly after collinear removal. Write the boundary
as $x=f(t)$, where $f$ is concave and $4-f(t)>0$. Then
\[
D_Y(t)=(4-f(t))^2+(Y-t)^2
\]
is strictly convex. If $Y_1<Y_2$ and $t_1,t_2$ are its respective minimizers,
adding their two minimizing inequalities gives
$(Y_2-Y_1)(t_2-t_1)\geq0$. Hence the projection's vertical coordinate never
decreases. Distances minimized over consecutive segment intervals decrease
then increase, with a possible tie at the shared minimizing vertex.
Start a pointer at the first segment. For each successive query advance
while the next segment distance is no larger; choose the last edge in a tie.
The pointer cannot move backwards and advances at most the chain length.
Each query and each advance costs constant exact squared-distance comparisons
by integer cross multiplication. Four rotated chains and their preparation
cost $O(m+k)$ arithmetic operations and linear space.
\end{proof}

The $O(\eta^{-1/2})$ order is classical worst-case optimal for approximating
\emph{all} planar support directions: an inscribed $2r$-vertex polygon has
an angular gap at least $\pi/r$, and a circle loses order $r^{-2}$ support
at that gap's midpoint. Fine rational polygons approximate the circle.
This is not a lower bound for computing one TV query.

\section{Event forests, relative error and bit cost}
\begin{definition}
An admitted event forest is an acyclic sum/product circuit with a fixed set
of original positions, or scope, at each node. A leaf lists distinct choices
at one position, or is an empty-scope unit/zero. Product scopes are disjoint
and their events form a Cartesian product. Sum alternatives have equal scopes
and disjoint assignment events. The root represents each member of
$\mathcal F$ once. Productions have neutral weights. Empty-scope nodes reduce
to unit/zero. There is no common-v-tree requirement.
\end{definition}

For each head independently let $D_i$ clear all denominators at position $i$,
and let $a_i(v)=D_i p_i(v)$; use separate denominators and $b_i$ for $q$.
Compute the exact positive integer root masses $A,B$ by ordinary inside
addition/multiplication. They are not inferred from compressed generators.
At a leaf keep pairs $(a_i(v),b_i(v))$; at a sum concatenate child lists;
at a product take all componentwise products of child pairs. Compress each
internal gate by Theorem~\ref{thm:planar}.
Core pairs may be signed: they represent norms, never probabilities or a
token sampler. The rounded head $Q$ remains the actual alternative law.
Exact unary unions and singleton diagonal maps may pass through without
compression. If omitted from the error count, they contribute zero error;
all experimental methods receive these same shortcuts.

Let $d$ be the maximum number of compression gates in an expanded root
derivation: a sum takes the maximum child count plus one; a binary product
adds both child counts plus one. Use $d\geq1$. Set
$k=\lceil\sqrt{d(1+\varepsilon)/(16\varepsilon)}\rceil$, computed by integer square root and
comparisons. At the root return
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 L=\frac{\sum_{(x,y)\in C}|Bx-Ay|}{2AB},
 \qquad U=\min\{1,(1+\varepsilon)L\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic rational forest certificate]\label{thm:forest}
The algorithm satisfies~\eqref{eq:target}, including TV zero exactly.
Let $H$ count explicit nodes and wires. Then $d\leq H$. With bounded fan-in
and bounded exact leaf lists, its core arithmetic cost and storage are
\[
 O\bigl(H(d/\varepsilon)\log(d/\varepsilon+2)\bigr),\qquad
 O\bigl(H\sqrt{d/\varepsilon}+d/\varepsilon\bigr).
\]
For general leaf lists of maximum size $V$, set
$M=64k$, $K_0=\max(V,M)$ and $J=HK_0+K_0^2$.
A conservative full bit bound is
\[
 \operatorname{poly}(\ell)+
 O\bigl(HJ\log(J+2)(\ell+\log(J+k+2))^2\bigr),
\]
where $\ell$ is total input bit length. No conditioning-mass or TV floor is
needed. Preparation of an input representation is charged separately.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If two child cores preserve all query norms from below by factors $\alpha$
and $\beta$, disjoint sum preserves their smaller factor. For a product,
fix each right generator and query the left norm with its componentwise
product with $w$. Sum the inequalities, then do the same for the right
norm using the retained left generators. This gives lower factor
$\alpha\beta$ and upper factor one, even with signed retained pairs.
Induction gives $(1-1/(16k^2))^d F_{\rm exact}\leq F_C\leq F_{\rm exact}$.
Bernoulli's inequality and $16k^2\geq d(1+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$ imply a
lower factor at least $1-\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)=1/(1+\varepsilon)$.
At query $w=(1/A,-1/B)$ the exact norm is $2\TV(P,Q)$, proving the interval.

A nonempty scoped node cannot repeat in a derivation: its lowest common
ancestor would be a product whose two disjoint scopes contain that same
nonempty scope. Acyclicity prevents repetition along a chain. This proves
$d\leq H$; repeated-squaring arithmetic DAGs are excluded by the scope contract.

Distinct-event mass at scope $I$ is at most $\prod_{i\in I}D_i$, since each
input row sums to at most one. Evaluate core containment on either coordinate
axis: the sum of absolute coordinates is at most that exact head mass.
This invariant also bounds product/union intermediates before compression.
Each coordinate therefore has $O(\ell)$ bits. Polygon vertices, cofactors,
scaled grid points and squared-distance comparisons have
$O(\ell+\log(J+k+2))$ bits, up to constant factors. Standard integer
multiplication gives the displayed conservative bit bound. Initial LCMs,
normalizers and final rational operations contribute polynomial input work.
At most $J$ candidates occur at a gate; Theorem~\ref{thm:planar} then bounds
every compression. Under bounded arities and leaf sizes products have $O(M^2)$
rows and sums $O(M)$, giving the sharper expressions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Products]\label{cor:products}
For two rational product measures on $n$ domains of size at most $q$, the
arithmetic cost is
\[
 O\bigl(nq\sqrt{n/\varepsilon}\log(q+2)\bigr)
\]
plus input/scaling/normalizer work, with polynomial-bit arithmetic.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Start with $(1,1)$. At position $i$, merge the diagonal images
$(a_i(v)x,b_i(v)y)$ for every original $v$ and every previous core pair
$(x,y)$. Compress once. Here $d\leq n$ and the intermediate list has $qM$
pairs; it does not form a wasteful general $M^2$ Cartesian product.
Each nonnegative diagonal map preserves canonical angular order, including
singular maps which collapse to one direction after omitting zero images.
An exact $q$-way heap merge costs $O(qM\log(q+2))$, aggregates collinear
images and supplies an ordered list to the linear compressor. No original
probability event is discarded: the list represents a support norm, not a
sampling law. The generic forest product may use signed diagonal maps and
still has the preceding, different complexity bound.
Normalizer masses are products of integer row sums. Apply the preceding
norm induction and sweep bound. For an empty product TV is zero.
\end{proof}

\section{A polynomial original-word grammar corollary}
The forest-input theorem is not a proof of a software normalizer.
Here is a separate mathematical full-input construction. Trim unreachable
and unproductive symbols of an LL(1) byte grammar. Keep unit/epsilon rules,
replace terminals in longer bodies by preterminals, and right-binarize with
fresh fixed-chain auxiliaries. This is linear in grammar symbol occurrences
$g$ and preserves derivations bijectively, without nullable-subset expansion.

Construct an acyclic byte-prefix trie per original token position with a
separate final-byte closure edge for each original ID. Prefix tokens close
to the next boundary while longer tokens continue within the current trie.
Aliases have distinct closure edges; their probabilities occur once on those
edges. Empty/unsupported emissions are invalid and keep their mass in the
input table. Complete lattice paths and original assignments are bijective.
Let $V_b,E_b$ be lattice vertices/edges; these are bounded by total declared
token-byte occurrences before sharing.

\begin{theorem}[LL(1) source-input certificate]\label{thm:grammar}
For the trimmed LL(1) source, original token domains and positive valid
masses above, a deterministic rational certificate~\eqref{eq:target} is
computed with total cost polynomial in $g,V_b,E_b,\ell,1/\varepsilon$.
No common parse tree or fixed maximum nesting depth is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a binary rule $A\to BC$ add same-span dependency $A\to B$ if $C$ is
nullable and $A\to C$ if $B$ is nullable; add unit dependencies as well.
Every dependency is a nullable-prefix left corner. A cycle collapses to
source left recursion, excluded by LL(1); auxiliary chains alone cannot cycle.

Process chart cells $(A,u,v)$ by increasing topological difference $v-u$
and then in dependency order. At $u=v$ use the unique empty derivation or
zero. At positive spans include matching byte edges, unit children and
binary splits at every $u\leq w\leq v$. Interior children have shorter
spans; endpoint children use a nullable unit and an already computed
same-span child. Hence the circuit is acyclic and has $O(gV_b^2)$ cells
and $O(gV_b^3+gE_b)$ alternatives.

Standard chart induction gives every path parse exactly once. LL(1)
unambiguity makes alternatives disjoint as original assignment events, not
just tree tags. Trimming ensures relevant nonterminal ambiguity cannot be
hidden outside the start language. Vertex layers determine consumed closure
slots, so sum scopes agree and product scopes partition. An empty scope stays
inside one trie, with a unique path and unambiguous parse, and reduces to
unit/zero. Theorem~\ref{thm:forest} applies. All source/lattice work is charged.
This is classical parsing used for admission, not a new parsing algorithm.
\end{proof}

\section{A software use: auditing numerical probability tables}
If a certified upper bound $U$ is below an application's error budget $\tau$,
replacing $P$ by $Q$ changes the expectation of \emph{every} verifier with
values in $[0,1]$ by at most $\tau$. This is the standard operational meaning
of TV; it requires no chosen semantic labels or favorable benchmark.
For numerical table storage, nominal FP64-to-FP32 storage halves and
FP64-to-16-bit storage quarters before metadata and transfers. We certify
the distributional effect, not model throughput or parameter quantization.

Multiplying each row by a positive constant does not change its conditioned
law: every valid assignment consumes each declared position exactly once,
so the common product of row constants cancels in its normalizer. A sampler
can therefore use integer-scaled rounded atom weights without carrying row
normalization constants through sampling. This is elementary and not our
algorithmic novelty; the certificate audits the resulting changed law.

A common continuation kernel cannot increase TV. For a finite iterative
decoder with uniform per-reachable-history certificates $\tau_t$, a coupling
gives final trajectory distance at most $\sum_t\tau_t$. This consequence
requires the same sampling/commitment policy. A confidence threshold that
changes under rounding needs a separate policy-preservation argument; local
head closeness alone does not prove trajectory closeness. One observed
history also does not certify every possible future history.

\begin{corollary}[Budgeted table substitution]\label{cor:budget}
Consider a finite-horizon decoder which samples a grammar-conditioned head
and applies a common update rule. Keep its original policy inputs and change
only the table used by the sampler. Start with budget $\tau\in[0,1]$. At each
processed history, admit a substitute only with a valid certificate $U$ no
larger than the remaining budget, then deduct $U$; otherwise keep the original
table and deduct zero. The resulting trajectory law differs from the original
by TV at most $\tau$, hence every final $[0,1]$-valued verifier changes in
expectation by at most $\tau$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
While histories match, maximally couple the two sampler laws. Their
disagreement probability at that step is at most the admitted $U$, also
after contraction under the common update. The probability of any first
disagreement is bounded by the expected sum of these certificates over the
matching prefix. Each substituted trajectory has nonnegative remaining
budget, so its full sum, and thus that prefix sum, is at most $\tau$.
The coupling inequality proves the conclusion.
\end{proof}

This standard coupling application does not require precomputing all future
heads: admission enforces the invariant whenever a history is reached.
Budget affects table admission only, not the common policy. Resource refusal
or unsupported input retains the original table; a zero reference valid mass
uses the same declared failure behavior. It does not automatically apply to
native EPIC or to changed confidence thresholds. No iterative neural run of
this wrapper, model quantization or generation-speed gain is measured here.

\subsection{Complete functional audit, not a competitive corpus}
The public packet contains an archived full MDLM head on a 19-slot canvas,
four free positions and all 50,258 original IDs. Its selection preceded the
independent A24 observations; it is the sole complete packet published offline.
No new model forward, changed source completion or semantic label is used.
We froze all three rounding representations (FP32, FP16, BF16), both
tolerances and all four core methods before measurement. Round \emph{probability tables}, then normalize
whole rows exactly as rationals; unsupported IDs and zeros are retained.
The admitted bridge uses immutable deterministic JSON lexer/predictive-stack
sources. Explicit stacks can grow exponentially; preparation is charged and
is not the polynomial chart implementation of Theorem~\ref{thm:grammar}.

Each exact normalizer is independently checked against the frozen stack
posterior. The forest contains \OriginalWordCount{} valid original-token
assignments. Enumerating that count is not attempted or used as a timeout
competitor. Independent tiny original-ID enumeration validates the bridge,
both masses and TV interval before the public audit.

The inexpensive controls use the same conditioned distributions.
If $a\leq Q/P\leq b$ on $P$'s support and $Q$ is supported there, the sharp
range upper bound is $(1-a)(b-1)/(b-a)$, with the equal-law case handled
separately. The Hellinger control uses
$\TV\leq\sqrt{1-(\sum_y\sqrt{P(y)Q(y)})^2}$; integer square-root intervals
on atom weights and scalar inside inference give a certified rational upper
bound. It does not rely on unverified floating-point cancellation.

The stronger controls handle lost support explicitly. Let $S$ be the set of
assignments with positive probability under both heads, $a=P(S)$, $b=Q(S)$,
and $P_S,Q_S$ the normalized restrictions when both masses are positive.
Couple the common mass first to obtain
\[
 \TV(P,Q)\leq\max(1-a,1-b)+\min(a,b)\TV(P_S,Q_S).
\]
Disjoint supports give TV exactly one. On the common support we combine
Hellinger, both inequalities
$\TV\leq\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2}$ and the sharp likelihood range. Integer
square-root intervals certify Hellinger. Each chi-square atom is rounded
upwards with 64 bits of \emph{relative} precision: for integer numerator
weight $u$, denominator weight $v>0$, compute
$\lceil 2^t u^2/v\rceil$, with $t=64+\operatorname{bitlength}(\max v)$
at that position. This avoids a poor control caused by denominator imbalance.
Scalar inside computations give certified conditional bounds; retaining the
best of them and the original controls never weakens the baseline.

An inexpensive composable norm control uses integer-cofactor normalization
with a support-point $u$ and maximum-determinant $v$, followed by exact angular
buckets on the unit-square boundary. A width $1/N$ yields at most $8N+4$
integer rows and all-query lower factor $1-20/N$. Use
$N=\lceil20d(1+\varepsilon)/\varepsilon\rceil$ to match the end-to-end guarantee.
Three replayable passes stream Cartesian candidates instead of refusing a
large materialization. The strongest version tries coarser grids and accepts
one only after exact certification of the minimum norm ratio over all original
and retained generator-perpendicular directions. Between such directions the
ratio is fractional linear, so checking the endpoints is sufficient.
These optimizations are classical, competent controls rather than new claims.
The endpoint, hybrid angular/endpoint and ordered endpoint methods retain
the same token laws and complete costs. Different nearest-edge ties can
yield slightly different valid intervals without invalidating either one.

\section{Related alternatives and exact claim boundaries}\label{sec:related}
Fu's general admissible-gate theorem already permits the disjoint union and
componentwise product operations of our forest~\cite{fu}. Therefore the
generic existence of deterministic approximation on an admitted forest is
a specialization, rather than our main discovery. The smaller integer core
and resulting quantitative bound are the claim of this draft. Specific
priority is based on the primary results inspected as of this draft's date,
not an exhaustive-literature guarantee.

Domain reduction for smooth decomposable probabilistic circuits requires a
common v-tree in its current theorem; determinism is not required~\cite{domain}.
A parse forest can have differing product splits. This distinction is not
an encoding-independent separation, and the more general admissible-gate
framework remains a competitor. Ordinary exact enumeration is preferable
for small valid sets. Certified Hellinger/KL or range bounds are preferable
when their cheaper upper guarantees suffice.

The ICML inference reduction~\cite{inference} also deserves a competent grammar
encoding. Normalize each reachable alternative by exact inside masses; activate
the children selected by their parents, and give unused nodes a canonical
inactive value. Decomposability precludes repeated nonempty nodes in a
derivation. Complete choice vectors and valid assignments are bijective.
Boolean activation gadgets avoid exponential CPT descriptions for shared nodes.
For the reduction's diagonal and topological-prefix queries, minimum local
conditional probabilities and clamped already active choices are ordinary
weighted inside operations. A visited node's activation is determined by
the prefix, so arbitrary future activation evidence is unnecessary.
This is a classical randomized alternative, not a newly claimed reduction
or a measured implementation. Its failure parameter and $\varepsilon^{-2}$
sample dependence differ from the deterministic bound here. Recent linear-time
randomized product algorithms are also stronger choices in their setting~\cite{linear}.

EPIC and commitment selectors perform different operations. This manuscript
neither evaluates EPIC's complete law nor claims generation superiority over
